A Serengeti Safari for All Senses

Hear the thunder of wildebeest on the run, lions roaring and hyenas laughing. Feel the goosebumps rise as huge herds of elephants walk past your jeep, a cheetah accelerates after its prey and a leopard slinks through the tall grass. And when it suddenly dawns on you how lucky you are to see all these wonders with your own eyes, don’t hold back the tears of joy.

Named Africa’s Leading National Park 2025, the Serengeti is also a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the backdrop to countless films and documentaries. Its name comes from the Maasai language and translates as ‘endless plains’ – which describes it well. Covering almost 15,000 km² (6,000 sq miles), Serengeti National Park ranks as Tanzania’s second-largest park.

Nearly all of Africa’s famous animals live here, the Big Five included, as well as rare species such as the pangolin and the African wild dog. The Serengeti offers diversity like nowhere else: eye-catching acacia trees scattered across the savanna, and a landscape that ranges from grasslands and mountains to swamps and lakes. Whether you choose the popular Northern Circuit, the Central Serengeti or the Western Corridor, every part of this special patch of land has its own wonders.

The Great Wildebeest Migration

Each year, millions of zebras, gazelles and wildebeests head for greener grazing. It may sound like a gentle stroll, but it is actually one of the Seven Natural Wonders of the World, full of danger, excitement and joy along the way. During their journey the animals must get across the Grumeti and Mara Rivers, with their rushing waters and huge crocodiles, while all the time trying to stay one step ahead of hungry predators. Then comes the calving season, when thousands of young are born every single day.

Best of all, this spectacle can be seen at any point in the year, because the animals move in a loop between the Masai Mara (Kenya) and the Serengeti. Exactly when they pass depends on the rainfall, which varies from year to year.

River Crossings

Love an adrenaline rush? Then the river crossings are what you’re after. They happen from June to September. The Grumeti River crossing in the Western Corridor draws fewer visitors yet is still spectacular, while the Mara River crossing is seen as the Northern Serengeti’s “main event” – complete with a large audience.

Predators and Calving

From January to March, the herds gather on the Ndutu plains and in the Southern Serengeti. Around 8,000 wildebeest are born each day in February, which also brings plenty of leopards, cheetahs and lions looking for an easier meal.

Photography

Between November and May, the rains turn the Serengeti into a green paradise. Colours are brighter, the air is clearer, and in November the migratory birds come back. It is an ideal time for photographers hunting for that one perfect shot. Combine it with a specially designed itinerary and your odds of getting it are good.

Whatever time of year you pick for a Serengeti migration visit, you won’t go home disappointed. Every season brings its own special moments that will leave you amazed.

The Big Five

Last but not least, the most famous group of animals on the planet: the Big Five. And yes, every one of them can be seen here. Despite what many people think, the name has nothing to do with them being the grandest animals of all. It actually comes from big game hunters, because these were the toughest animals to track down. The more you know 😉

Here is a closer look at the Serengeti’s five superstars.

Lion

The star of The Lion King and the unchallenged king of the savanna. You’ll often spot them on the granite outcrops known as Simba Kopjes, doing what cats are best at: sleeping. The Serengeti has large prides of lions, whose cubs play just like your cat does at home.

Elephant

These grey, mostly gentle giants are hard to miss yet surprisingly hard to hear. For all their size, elephants move remarkably quietly, particularly through the Lobo Valley. The herd’s biggest bull may look like the leader, but the real power and wisdom rest with the oldest female.

Leopard

Leopards are classic cats, just like lions. They enjoy sleeping, ideally up in trees, where they can vanish like Houdini. How they differ from cheetahs: they carry rosettes rather than spots, and they are far heavier. Now and then they simply drop out of a tree to catch their prey.

Rhino

Technically, the Big Five includes only the black rhino and not the white rhino (which was apparently easier to hunt). The white rhino has a broad lip, whereas the black rhino’s is pointed. The names have nothing to do with colour: most likely “white” is a mistranslation of the Afrikaans/Dutch word wyd (meaning “wide”), describing its broad lip, which English speakers took to mean the colour. The other species was then called “black” just to contrast with it.

Buffalo

Ernest Hemingway, novelist and safari lover, said the African buffalo “looks at you as if you owe him money.” That is rather apt: famously short-tempered, it is regarded as one of the most dangerous members of the Big Five. It would also win any staring contest.

A Serengeti safari is full of adventure, so you need to rest well and in comfort. After all, there is nothing better than a good night’s sleep with the sounds of the savanna all around you.

It depends on what you’d like to see. In the dry season (June to October and December to February), wildlife is easier to spot because the animals gather at waterholes more often, and the weather is nicer too. The drawback: it is peak season, so there are more tourists and hotel prices are higher.

From March to November, the mid season gives you a good balance of fewer visitors and moderate hotel rates. The weather can change, and because of the rains some attractions may have limited availability, but the wildlife sightings are still excellent and the safari feels more relaxed.

If you’re watching your budget, the wet season in April and May is ideal, as hotel rates are at their lowest. The heavy rains turn the landscape lush and vivid, though the weather can disrupt travel plans.

Because the park is so large, plan at least two days to really get a feel for the Serengeti. It may feel a little hurried, but you’ll see the highlights and, with luck, all of the Big Five.

Ideally, take three to four days so you can explore the Central Serengeti plus one other seasonal area, such as the South for the calving or the North for the river crossings.

With five to seven days, you can take your time to get the perfect photo or wait for a particular hunt or river crossing.

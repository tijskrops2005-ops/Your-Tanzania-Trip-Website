With the Serengeti, Mount Kilimanjaro and a wealth of living cultures, Tanzania draws adventurous travellers with its remarkable landscapes and wide variety of experiences. Before you go and see it all with your own eyes, why not set off on a “literary safari” first? Books about Tanzania give you a richer understanding of the country's nature, history and people. Here is our pick of the best Tanzania books, each of which captures the real character of this fascinating country.

Our three favourite books on Tanzanian history and culture

  • Uhuru Street: Short Stories by M.G. Vassanji: These short stories take place in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania’s busy port city, and open a window onto the lives of its many different communities. In his moving narratives, Vassanji examines cultural identity, social change and the legacy of colonialism, revealing the complexities of Tanzanian society.
  • Paradise by Abdulrazak Gurnah: Through the tale of a young boy torn between two cultures, this Nobel Prize-winning novel explores Tanzania's colonial past. Gurnah writes in lyrical prose that brings Zanzibar’s rich cultural heritage to life and shows how colonialism shaped the lives of the people who lived there.
  • The Hadza: Hunter-Gatherers of Tanzania by Frank Marlowe: In this anthropological study you get an absorbing look at the Hadza, one of the last hunter-gatherer societies still living in Africa. Marlowe's careful observations shed light on their traditional lifestyle, their bond with the land around them and their distinctive cultural practices.

Best Books About Tanzanian Nature

  • The Tree Where Man Was Born by Peter Matthiessen: Part travel writing, part natural history, this book places the Serengeti ecosystem centre stage and examines the intricate ties between animals and their surroundings. Matthiessen's vivid prose conveys the sheer scale of the Serengeti’s plains, from the thunder of the Great Wildebeest Migration to the quiet elegance of lone predators.
  • Wildlife of East Africa by Martin B. Withers and David Hosking: Wildlife enthusiasts should look no further, as this book gives detailed information about the species you may come across on safari. Combining striking photographs with informative text, it helps you identify animals, make sense of their behaviour and appreciate Tanzania's immense biodiversity.
  • Serengeti: The Eternal Beginning by Nigel Pavitt: A large-format coffee table book and a feast for the eyes, celebrating the extraordinary beauty of Serengeti National Park. Its stunning photographs convey the singular landscape, the drama of encounters between animals and the fragile ecosystem that flourishes in this one-of-a-kind environment.

Captivating fiction set in Tanzania

  • The Shadow of Kilimanjaro by Ernest Hemingway: In this collection of short stories, Hemingway's unmistakable style and his fascination with Africa are on full display. Seen through his characters' eyes, the reader encounters both the beauty and the harsh realities of life in Tanzania, confronting courage, loss and the power of nature along the way.
  • A Girl Called Problem by Katie Quirk: A coming-of-age tale about Lilia, a young Maasai girl who dreams of becoming a doctor. With the Masai Mara as its setting, the novel looks at cultural tradition, gender roles and the pursuit of one's own ambitions.
  • Afterlives by Abdulrazak Gurnah: This Booker Prize-shortlisted novel weaves together the lives of three characters from different generations, tracing the lasting effects of colonialism and personal loss. Moving between Tanzania and Germany, it reflects on memory, identity and the longing to belong.

Acclaimed Books by Tanzanian Authors

For a genuinely authentic perspective, it is worth turning to Tanzanian authors themselves. Their distinctive voices open a window onto local culture, traditions and present-day concerns. These are the three we recommend most:

  • S.J. Majele: A well-known poet whose writing takes on social justice, questions of identity and the intricacies of Tanzanian life.
  • Euphrase Kezilahabi: One of the leading names in Tanzanian literature, Kezilahabi wrote novels and poems that tackle political corruption, postcolonial struggles and the search for meaning.
  • Amina Yahya: Written by a prize-winning author, these children’s books are full of colourful tales that honour Tanzanian culture and pass on uplifting messages to a young audience.

Learn the Tanzanian Language

When you are preparing for a trip to Tanzania, picking up a handful of basic Swahili phrases can make your time in the country even more rewarding. It helps with simple communication, of course, but it also shows respect for the local culture. There is a further bonus: it can lead to warmer connections with the people you meet, broad smiles included. The following books can help you on your way:

  • Say It in Swahili by Hugo Gysels: An accessible guide that lays the groundwork in Swahili vocabulary and grammar, which makes it well suited to beginners. Inside you find practical phrases for everyday situations, cultural notes and tips on pronunciation.
  • Pimsleur Swahili: An audio-based language course that uses its own distinctive method to immerse you in conversational Swahili. Because it concentrates on the spoken language and repetition, you build practical speaking skills and sharpen your listening comprehension.
  • Memrise: An online platform that turns language learning into a game, which keeps it fun and engaging. Its interactive lessons and spaced repetition system let you pick up Swahili vocabulary and grammar at whatever pace suits you.

These books on Tanzania make a wonderful starting point, but planning a trip is also the ideal excuse to watch documentary after documentary and film after film. Keep in mind that setting off with a “literary suitcase” full of stories enriches your travels in more ways than one. It helps you connect more deeply with the land and its people, and builds empathy and understanding.

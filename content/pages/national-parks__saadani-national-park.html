Saadani is a national park that many travellers pass by. Its setting on the coast makes it special: it is the only national park where an adventurous safari can be paired with a relaxed stay on lovely white, palm-fringed beaches, letting you watch wildlife in a truly unusual environment. No other national park in East Africa sits on the coast. Here the beach meets the bush, in the only wildlife sanctuary in East Africa with a beachfront on the Indian Ocean.

The park also has everything that makes Tanzania’s tropical coast and islands such a draw for European sun-seekers. At the same time, it is the one spot where a lazy hour of sunbathing might be interrupted by an elephant wandering past, or a lion arriving for a drink at the waterhole nearby. The scenery is varied too: mangroves, extensive coral reefs, sea and palm-lined beach, savannah and cotton fields.

Wildlife in Saadani National park

Four of the Big Five live in Saadani national park, though spotting them is far from easy, as numbers are low and the animals tend to stay hidden. The park borders local villages, and the wildlife does its best to avoid contact with people, whom it regards as a threat.

There is a population of Masai giraffes here as well. You will also find Lichtenstein’s hartebeest, common waterbucks and bohor reedbucks, blue wildebeests, common and red duikers and Dik-Diks. Then there are yellow baboons, vervet and blue sykes monkeys, plus Colobus and White-bearded emperor tamarin monkeys, mongooses, genets and porcupines. Greater kudus, elands, sable antelopes, warthogs, hippos, crocodiles and nile monitors call Saadani national park home too. African bush elephants, lions and leopards keep a lower profile in this park, but with a little luck you may see them as well. Herds of as many as 30 elephants are being seen more and more often, and several lion prides live here alongside leopards, spotted hyenas and black-backed jackals.

The protected area has been recognised as a Lion Conservation Unit since 2005. Along the Wami River you can find the beautiful mangrove kingfisher and the lesser flamingo, while the beaches are among the last important breeding grounds for green turtles on mainland Tanzania.

Excursions in Saadani National Park

A boat safari on the mangrove-fringed Wami River is one of the things to do in Saadani National Park; expect hippos, enormous crocodiles and the beautiful mangrove kingfisher and lesser flamingo.

A walking safari is another pleasant option here: you cross the park on foot in the company of an armed ranger.

Alternatively, pay a visit to Saadani fishing village inside the reserve, where a cluster of ruins bears witness to its 19th century heyday as an important trading port.

Just off the park's coast lies a sandbank that you can reach by boat for snorkelling, swimming, lazing about and soaking up the tropical setting. It only emerges at low tide, so the trip needs to be timed accordingly.

When to visit Saadani National Park

Wildlife watching in Saadani is at its best after the rains, during January and February and from June to August, when the plains still hold plenty of water and the animals remain inside the park.

Steer clear of the rainy season in April-May and November.

Information and facts

Elephants taking a dip in the ocean. Lions and hyenas out hunting. Countless kinds of monkeys leaping through the trees. Many members of the African animal kingdom live in Saadani National Park, even if they are also good at staying out of sight. All the same, the park is a real highlight, because safari adventure and beach relaxation blend here without a seam.

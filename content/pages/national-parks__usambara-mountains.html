The Usambara range is a lovely mountain area of grassy plains and untouched tropical rainforest, dotted with colourful villages that you can explore while you are here. You will find the Usambara mountains in the north-east of Tanzania, close to Mkomazi National Park and roughly a 3-hour drive (110 km) west of Tanga.

This is still one of Tanzania’s undiscovered corners, and that is precisely what makes it such a paradise for walkers and nature lovers. The green, lush mountains enjoy a cool climate that suits walking and hiking perfectly. Yet the strongest argument for hiking in the Usambaras is the view: wherever you go, you look out over rolling hills, mountains, valleys, farmland, pretty villages, colourful markets and plantations.

The Usambara mountains form the easternmost ranges of the Eastern Arc Mountains. The range stretches 90 km in length and is roughly half as wide. It was created by faulting and uplift almost two million years ago and consists of Precambrian metamorphic rock.

Birds in Usambara

Within East Africa the Usambara Mountains are quite exceptional, as their natural areas are still clad in tropical forest. That makes them ecologically hugely important and a recognised biodiversity hotspot with numerous protected zones. The Usambara forests are home to a range of species, among them the Usambara eagle-owl, the Usambara akalat, the Usambara weaver and the African violet. Other birds you may spot include the Mountain buzzard, Crown eagle, Eastern bronze-naped pigeon, Lemon and Dusky turtle dove and the Hartlaub’s Turaco.

Plants and wildlife of the Usambara mountains

More than 3000 plant species and over 600 tree species flourish in the Usambara mountains, which is why the area counts as one of the world’s biodiversity hotspots. Naturalists and nature enthusiasts travel here from across the globe to watch birds, study trees and flowers, or look for butterflies. A number of species occur only in the Usambara forests.

Hiking activities

Join a local guide for a hike and discover the wonderful variety of this huge area. When the sky is clear you can make out the grass plains of Mkomazi and even Kilimanjaro, while taking in the lovely views the region offers. Along the way you will meet local people who wave and greet you with a warm smile. They are very welcoming, and if you show openness and interest you may well find yourself spontaneously invited to get to know them better.

A climb to the Irente viewpoint rewards you with a panorama over Mazinde village nearly 1000 m below and the wide Maasai plains stretching beyond it.

Alternatively, walk through the fertile farmland of Jaegertal to a fruit tree nursery, where you learn about the various kinds of fruit trees and how they are propagated. The “Growing Rock” tour sets off from Soni and takes you to the summit of Kwamongo mountain, known for its butterflies, with views from the top over Soni, Lushoto and the Handeni plains.

Best time to visit Usambara Mountains

The climate is pleasant all year, but in the rainy season from March through May the trails become too muddy for trekking. June to October is the best period to come, once the rains have passed and the air is at its clearest.

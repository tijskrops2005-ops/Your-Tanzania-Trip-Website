Surfing in Tanzania: Exploring the Coastal Surfing Havens

11 July 2024

If you are an experienced surfer, “Tanzania” may not be the first place that comes to mind when you picture sun-drenched beaches and barrelling waves. Still, tucked along the country’s beautiful coastline is a lively and growing surf scene for anyone prepared to look beyond the usual spots. Whether it’s the world-class breaks of Pangani or the hidden gems of the Southern Zanzibar Archipelago, surfing in Tanzania is a distinctive and memorable experience. Read on to find out more about surfing in Tanzania, then pick up your board and set off on your next adventure!

Top Spots for Surfing in Tanzania

With more than 800 kilometres of coastline, much of which mass tourism has never reached, Tanzania has a great many undiscovered surf breaks. Put simply, you get a feeling of adventure and exclusivity that few other surf destinations can offer.

Along the coast you’ll find a patchwork of varied landscapes, ranging from the striking cliffs and beautiful white sand beaches of the Tanzanian mainland to the coral reefs and idyllic atolls of Zanzibar. As a result, there are waves of every kind for surfers of every level.

Pangani

North of Dar es Salaam, the relaxed coastal town of Pangani is regarded as the “crown jewel of Tanzanian surfing”. Steady swells arriving from the Indian Ocean produce long, peeling right-hand point breaks that suit intermediate and advanced surfers perfectly. The well-known “The Point” break is a dream for surfers, with long rides and demanding manoeuvres. For a more laid-back atmosphere, head to nearby beaches such as Utafta, where the softer waves also work well for beginners.

Away from the surf, Pangani has a charm that suits the surfing experience well. Its long history shows in the traditional Swahili buildings and lively local markets. For wildlife and culture, make your way to the Pangani River, a refuge for birdlife and the location of historical sites such as the Pangani Caves.

Zanzibar

Zanzibar, Tanzania’s well-known archipelago, combines cultural immersion with unspoilt surfing conditions in a way you won’t find elsewhere. Several hidden surf gems lie at the island’s southern tip, around Paje and Michamvi. These are mostly reef breaks, producing shorter yet powerful waves that are best for experienced surfers. Turquoise water and swaying palms make the setting idyllic, which only adds to the appeal of surfing in Zanzibar.

Besides the surf, Zanzibar is a cultural melting pot shaped by influences from Arabia, Africa and the Far East. The island’s historic capital, Stone Town, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. On a tour of the town you can get a sense of Zanzibar’s rich history: wander its narrow alleys, browse the busy spice markets and admire its remarkable architecture.

More surf spots in Tanzania worth a visit

Pangani and Zanzibar may be Tanzania’s best-known surf destinations, but the long coastline has plenty more to offer surfers with a sense of adventure. Below is a quick look at a few more spots that are waiting to be explored.

Mainland Tanzania

  • Dar es Salaam: The busy city of Dar es Salaam has a handful of surfable spots right on its doorstep. Oyster Bay (Coco Beach), on the peninsula, impresses beginners and intermediates with reliable beach breaks. If you have more experience, try Sea Cliff, a demanding reef break on the city’s northern tip.
  • Southern Coast: Further down the coast, close to the town of Lindi, you’ll find Mchinga Beach. Its long, rolling waves make this remote spot ideal for intermediate surfers and longboarders. Do be ready for very basic infrastructure and a genuinely off-the-grid experience, though.

Zanzibar Archipelago

  • Dongwe Beach: On Zanzibar’s east coast, Dongwe Beach has a powerful right-hand reef break that is ideal for experienced surfers. Because of the steady winds, getting out to the break can be tricky and often means taking a boat.
  • Nungwi: Nungwi, a popular spot with tourists, has waves suited to beginners, so it works well if you are new to surfing. With its lovely beaches and gentle waves, it is an excellent place to learn while taking in the beautiful scenery.
  • Sinda Islands: Lying off Pemba Island, another island in the Zanzibar Archipelago, this group of islands offers you a secluded and distinctive surfing experience. Experienced surfers will find demanding waves on the reef breaks, and the remote location keeps the surroundings untouched and pristine.

Hidden Gems

Along Tanzania’s long coastline there are countless possible surf spots still to be found. There are many rumours of hidden reef breaks offshore, especially in the Northern Zanzibar area. Getting to these spots usually takes local knowledge and a chartered boat, which adds to the adventurous side of surfing in Tanzania.

Keep in mind that a lot of Tanzania’s surf scene is still taking shape, so facilities and infrastructure may be more limited than in better-known destinations. But if you are planning your trip to Tanzania hoping for an authentic experience and the excitement of exploring unknown waters, surfing in Tanzania will suit you!

Surf with care and get to know the culture

There is more to surfing in Tanzania than riding waves. It gives you the chance to immerse yourself in a distinctive culture and to support responsible tourism. The four tips below make it easy to have a positive impact.

  • Support local businesses: Pick locally owned restaurants, guest houses and surf camps so your money goes straight to the local community.
  • Respect the environment: Think about how you dispose of your waste and don’t leave litter in the sea or on the beaches (this one goes without saying).
  • Engage with the local community: Pick up a few phrases in Swahili, treat local people with respect and take an interest in their culture.
  • Support sustainable practices: Go for lodges and surf camps that put sustainability first, for example through water conservation and waste reduction.

Following these practices helps Tanzania’s surf tourism grow responsibly, so its beauty and cultural richness are protected for generations to come.

Still in its early days, Tanzania’s surf scene gives you a feeling of adventure and discovery if you want something off the beaten path. Thanks to its varied coastline, quiet breaks and rich cultural heritage, Tanzania could become a leading surf destination. As the surfing community expands and responsible practices take hold, the country is well placed to create its own niche in the world of surfing, offering a memorable experience to surfers of every level.

Whether you are an experienced wave rider looking for a fresh challenge or a curious beginner wanting to try the sport – there is something for everyone in Tanzania’s up-and-coming surf scene. So pack your boards, embrace the adventure and get ready to find the hidden gems among Tanzania’s surf spots.

More than 30,000 people try to climb Kilimanjaro each year, and for many of them it is an experience that happens only once in a lifetime.

To give anyone thinking about Kilimanjaro trekking the information they need, we’ve written this beginner’s guide to climbing Mount Kilimanjaro.

Kilimanjaro is harder to climb than you may expect. That is why being fully prepared for your trek is essential, and that includes knowing how to climb it and when the best time is.

You also need to be sure that you bring the right equipment. Good gear and knowing your destination will keep you safe on the way up.

Here is our complete beginner’s guide to trekking Kilimanjaro. Take our advice on board and get ready to tackle the mountain.

Where Is Kilimanjaro?

You will find Kilimanjaro in the northeast of the country, near the border between Kenya and Tanzania. The nearest city is Arusha and the nearest airport is Kilimanjaro International Airport (airport code JRO).

Tanzania’s dormant volcano, known to us as Kilimanjaro volcano, is made up of three volcanic cones — Kibo, Mawenzi and Shira.

How High Is Mount Kilimanjaro?

Kilimanjaro rises 5,895 meters (19,340 ft) above sea level. It is the highest point in Africa as well as the tallest freestanding mountain in the world.

When in the year should you climb Mount Kilimanjaro?

The best moment to climb Kilimanjaro is during one of the two dry seasons. Because of where it lies in Tanzania, the area has just two seasons: wet and dry.

From July to October it is mostly dry. The second dry season falls between December and February. Both periods are excellent for trekking Mount Kilimanjaro.

Experienced travellers could attempt Kilimanjaro trekking in the wet season, though we strongly advise against it. Your chances of failing are higher, so it is better to wait for the drier seasons, which are safer too.

How many days does a Kilimanjaro climb take?

Trekking Mount Kilimanjaro takes anywhere from 5 to 9 days, depending on the route you choose.

Kilimanjaro has 8 recognised routes: Umbwe, The Western Breach, Machame, Lemosho, Shira, Rongai, Northern Circuit and Shira. Each of them starts from a different point on the mountain.

It is widely known that half of all climbers do not succeed. You can prevent this by choosing a longer route – longer routes are known to raise the Kilimanjaro trekking success rate considerably.

The routes with the best results take 8 to 9 days and have success rates of 85% to 95%.

Some tour guides recommend the five-day Marangu Route, often called the “Tourist” route, as it is the cheapest and “easiest” choice.

Sadly, this route has a low success rate, entirely due to altitude acclimatization.

The reason is that climbers do not get enough time to adjust to the thin air as they go higher up the mountain. It remains popular, however, because there are huts to sleep in along the way.

Even so, the final section is very steep, which makes finishing the climb hard.

Why do people consider Kilimanjaro trekking hard?

You can certainly trek Kilimanjaro, even though it is regarded as a tough challenge. The trails offer little exposure and little risk of injury from falling. There are also no technical climbs and no steep drops.

Apart from a few short stretches where you need to scramble (using hands and feet), the climb is simply hiking.

What makes it hard is the altitude. At the summit of Kilimanjaro, the air contains roughly half the oxygen found at sea level.

Working with little oxygen puts the body under stress. It has to adapt so it can keep going at high altitude without falling ill. Climbing gradually is the key to lowering the risk of altitude sickness.

Do you need a guide to climb Mount Kilimanjaro?

Every climber on Mount Kilimanjaro must be accompanied at all times by a licensed guide from Kilimanjaro National Park.

Most climbers have a team made up of a lead guide, an assistant guide, a cook and porters. Whatever the set-up, you must always have a guide with you and do as they say.

Having walked the route hundreds of times, they know best how to keep you and everyone else safe.

Temperatures and campsites on Mount Kilimanjaro

Some people think Kilimanjaro is never cold, since it lies in Africa, a continent linked with very hot weather.

In fact, the summit of the mountain is very cold and windy, particularly at night. The lowest temperatures here usually range between 20 and -20 degrees Celsius.

So if you take your Kilimanjaro trekking adventure seriously, you need to bring the right clothing to protect you from the cold. Thermals are essential, and so is a pair of sturdy walking boots.

On a Kilimanjaro climb you mostly sleep in a tent. One route has huts to sleep in, but camping is much more usual.

Although you will often be camping, campfires are not allowed on the mountain. There is simply not enough wood to sustain open fires. Food is cooked on propane burners.

At every campsite there are public toilets that are cleaned regularly. When a camp is crowded, though, they can get rather dirty. Some companies may supply you with a portable toilet.

Lastly, there are no facilities at all (such as washrooms or showers) while trekking Kilimanjaro. If you like, you can pack baby wipes to freshen up. Most people simply cope with going without a shower for up to 9 days.

Gear Required To Trek Up Kilimanjaro

You definitely need quite a lot of gear to climb Kilimanjaro. The climb starts in the rainforest and ends in the Arctic zone at the summit, crossing several distinct ecological zones along the way.

Your mountain clothing must keep you warm and dry and shield you from the elements when it is cold and wet.

You will need gear, good footwear and technical clothing made from waterproof, insulated and breathable fabrics. That also means a duffel bag, a sleeping bag and a day pack.

Check your agreement with the company you book with to find out whether you have to bring your own tent.

Cost To Climb Mount Kilimanjaro 

Across the industry, the cost of trekking Mount Kilimanjaro has risen sharply. The 8-day Lemosho Route, one of the most popular routes, costs around $3,000 on average.

Park fees on their own account for most of the cost, with staff wages coming next.

Low-quality budget operators may offer this climb for under $2,000 too. Premium operators charge up to $6,000 for the same service.

Never pick an operator simply because they offered a “good bargain” or because their website “looks good”.

The climb may only be completed by trekkers whose guides are trained or have access to emergency medical care.

The best operators consistently get excellent reviews from clients and are respected by others in the industry.

Doing your research and comparing options is essential. Here, the cheapest choice is not always the right one.

Vaccinations And Travel Insurance 

Visitors to Tanzania are advised to be vaccinated against typhoid, hepatitis A and tetanus.

Malaria risk is high in most of Tanzania, so anti-malaria medication is recommended and you must take measures to avoid mosquito bites.

To avoid mosquito bites, wear long sleeves and long trousers, particularly after dark, put insect repellent on any bare skin and sleep under a mosquito net.

Because Kilimanjaro is so high above sea level, there are practically no mosquitoes on the mountain. Kilimanjaro is nonetheless regarded as part of the rainforest region.

You also need to make sure you are covered by travel insurance. Your policy must include cover for high-altitude hiking (up to 6,000 meters).

It simply protects you if something goes wrong while you are on the mountain. Although nothing should happen during your trip, having it just in case is very important.

Mkomazi National Park is a splendid national park in the north of Tanzania. Established in 1951, it is remote and was initially hard to get to. Unlike the other parks, it never received the funding it needed, and its real value was only acknowledged in 1989.

Nowadays Mkomazi National Park is easy to reach, and it is a genuinely spectacular wilderness! Look to the northwest and you can see Kilimanjaro, the highest mountain in Africa, while the Pare and Usambara mountains form a dramatic backdrop to the south. Along the northern boundary lies Kenya’s Tsavo National Park. Together the two parks make up one of the largest and most important protected ecosystems, serving as shared territory for herds of elephants, oryx and zebras. Mkomazi sits at the southern tip of the Sahel. The landscape is classic dry country, with grey-green nyika bush, ancient baobabs and isolated rocky hills. Elsewhere the sea of scrub gives way to savannah woodland with umbrella acacias and mbugas, the flat grassy valleys.

Wildlife living in Mkomazi

Wildlife in Mkomazi National Park is sparse and fairly shy. The species are typical of this dry landscape: giraffes, antelopes, lesser kudu, impala and Grant’s gazelle. Alongside them live elephants, buffalo and a good number of predators, among them lion, leopard and cheetah. With a bit of luck a game drive will regularly turn up other animals too, such as dik-diks, zebras, wildebeest, eland and topi. In total, 78 recorded mammal species live in the park.

Birds in the national park

More than 400 bird species have been registered in the park. These include specialities of the dry north such as Shelley’s starling and the three-streaked tchagra, which makes it a superb destination for birdwatchers! Hornbills, weaver birds, guinea fowl, martial eagles and the purple Eurasian hoopoe also live here. Mkomazi is moreover the only place in Tanzania where you have a good chance of seeing the striking vulturine guineafowl. Large birds that are fairly common on the grassy plains include ostrich, kori bustard, secretary bird and southern ground hornbill. On top of that, migrants such as the European roller are around from November to April.

Mkomazi Rhino Sanctuary

In 1989 the Tanzanian government asked the George Adamson Wildlife Preservation Trust to work with it, and as a result the endangered black rhino was reintroduced into a heavily guarded, fenced section of the park: the Mkomazi Rhino Sanctuary.

In the midst of the rhino poaching crisis, “protecting the fortresses” is an important part of the job. Yet winning over the local communities is just as vital if Mkomazi is never again to lose its entire rhino population. In 2012, Prince William came to Mkomazi National Park to see for himself how conservation is carried out on the front line of a Tanzanian national park.

The aim of the Mkomazi Rhino Sanctuary is to establish a viable breeding group of 50 black rhinos, whose young will go on to reinforce the existing populations elsewhere in Tanzania. The rhinos living in the sanctuary represent almost 30% of all black rhinos in Tanzania, which makes them enormously important, both for the country and for the future of this vulnerable species. While he was in Mkomazi, Prince William also visited the Mkomazi education programme at Rafiki Wa Faru, where children from 14 nearby villages learn about rhinos and other wildlife, in the hope that poachers will never again wipe out the rhinos of Mkomazi.

When to visit Mkomazi National Park

For general wildlife viewing, the Dry season from June to October is the best time in the park. That said, Mkomazi isn’t primarily a wildlife destination, and the scenery and the mountain views are at their finest in the Wet season, from November to May. This is also when you have the best chance of seeing elephants migrating into the park from Tsavo.

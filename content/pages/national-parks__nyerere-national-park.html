Formerly known as the Selous Game Reserve, Nyerere National Park ranks among the biggest protected areas on the planet. You will find it in the south of Tanzania. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1982, thanks to the variety of wildlife and the untouched nature it holds. Even though it lies a long way from the national parks in the north of Tanzania, it is well worth the journey. Covering 50,000 km2, plus extra buffer zones, it is larger than Denmark!

Wildlife in Nyerere

On safari in Nyerere National Park you can come across an enormous number of wild animals. The park is home to lions, leopards, hippos and crocodiles. You may see endangered black rhinos, elusive East African wild dogs and hyenas too. Cape buffaloes, Masai giraffes, wildebeests and zebras also live here; the last two are famous for the great wildebeest migration! Naturally there are impalas as well, together with large herds of Lichtenstein’s hartebeests, waterbucks and sable antelope, plus elands and bushbucks. Because Nyerere is much quieter than the other parks, a truly unique experience on your Tanzania holiday is well within reach! In the past the park sheltered a very large population of African bush elephants. Sadly, heavy poaching in the game reserve has caused their numbers to drop sharply.

Birds in the Nyerere National Park

Thousands of birds live in Nyerere National Park. Researchers have counted more than 445 bird species across the park's different habitats. Near the lakes, for example, you will find the giant kingfisher, the pink-backed pelican and the African skimmer. On the sandbanks look out for the white-fronted bee-eater, fish eagle, carmine, ibises and palmnut vultures. Other water birds you may spot in Nyerere National Park include the purple-crested turaco, yellow-billed stork, trumpeter hornbill, white-crowned spur-winged plovers, malachite kingfishers and a range of small waders, alongside migratory species.

Boat tour in Nyerere

Nyerere is one of only a handful of national parks that offer boat safaris, and one of the few where walking safaris are permitted. If you would like to take in the lovely scenery of the Rufiji River, with its many hippos and crocodiles and its rich birdlife, this is the perfect outing. Sunset from the boat can be breathtaking as well – few things match the calm of the river as the sun sinks below the horizon.

Best time to visit

The dry season, from June to October, is the best time for Nyerere. Animals are easier to see then, as the vegetation is thinner and wildlife gathers around the familiar water sources. Towards the end of the heavy rains the roads become almost impossible to use, and many camps shut as the wet season draws to a close. If you hope to see the rare wild dogs, their denning period from June to August is a good time to go.

Advice from our experts on Nyerere

I am Nixon, and I have been working as a guide in Nyerere for 16 years. Most of our guests are struck by how many animals live close to the water, such as the hippos and crocodiles. Our clients really enjoy a walk to the Stiegler Gorge, which makes a nice break from the jeep safari.

How many days should you stay? Plan at least 3 days, counting your arrival and departure days, since neither of those is a full day. That way only the second day is a complete one.

A few tips on the best time and way to get there:

  • Stay away during the wet months; my advice is to go between June and October, as the park cannot be reached in the rainy season. It turns into a swamp, and even the animals move away to Mikumi or into the depths of the park.
  • Nyerere lies a long way from every other park; each of them is a full day's drive away at the very least. I would recommend flying, which I believe is the only comfortable option.

Landscape in Nyerere

The whole reserve is crossed by the Rufiji River, which reaches the Indian Ocean across from Mafia Island. The river is surrounded by swampland where huge numbers of crocodiles live. One of the highlights here is the Stiegler Gorge, a chasm 100 metres deep and 100 metres wide.

If you love travel and adventure, Tanzania is where you want to be. This lovely East African country on the Indian Ocean has something to offer every kind of traveller. Between its big cities and untouched landscapes, you can fulfil all your holiday wishes without ever leaving this sunny oasis.

To help you put together the ideal trip, we share advice on planning, on where to go and on tricks that help you make the most of your travels.

About Tanzania

Tanzania is a country of extremes. It lies 600 feet (or 200 metres) above sea level and is home to both Africa’s highest mountain and the world’s second deepest lake. Indeed, Kilimanjaro, measuring 19,340 feet (or 5,895 metres), stands in this country.

Lake Tanganyika, at 4,710 feet (or 1,436 metres), is the second deepest lake on earth. First place belongs to Russia’s Lake Baikal at 5,387 feet (or 1,642 metres), which leaves a big gap between the two.

Still, the fact that these are the country’s best-known features does not mean they are the only delights you can enjoy.

History Of Tanzania

In 1964, the mainland, Tanganyika, joined with the islands of Zanzibar. That union marked the birth of the United Republic of Tanzania.

The union could only happen once both countries had gained independence from Britain. Tanganyika became independent in 1961, whereas Zanzibar only achieved it in January 1964. Three months after that, Tanzania was united.

Because it holds the oldest human fossils in the world, Tanzania is called “the Cradle of Mankind”. The country has 120 different indigenous groups, and as there was very little emigration or colonial settlement in its past, much of its original history has been preserved.

Language

Tanzania has two official languages – Swahili and English. Many people in the country speak English, but it is only taught in higher education. Swahili is taught from the first 7 years of school right through to the end of a pupil’s education.

As the country is so large and culturally varied, locals also speak many unofficial languages. Many of the 120 ethnic groups have a language of their own, for instance, and Asian influences have made Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi and Gujarati widely spoken.

Culture

Family and kinship are central to Tanzania’s culture. Whatever the difficulty or inconvenience, family matters and events always come first. Transport in Tanzania leaves a lot to be desired, yet that will not keep a relative from turning up for a celebration.

Art and history also matter greatly in Tanzania’s culture. Most entertainment comes from storytelling through speech and dance. And since scientists from all over the world study human ancestry in Tanzanian soil, history and nature are closely intertwined.

Planning Your Trip

When you plan a trip to Tanzania, the two key things to consider are the weather and the experience you want.

Why Should You Visit Tanzania?

Tanzania is full of wildlife, beaches and archaeological finds, with a culture that globalisation has largely left untouched.

Later on we go into detail about what you can do in this unique country, but the main reason to spend time in Tanzania is to discover a world that is still untouched.

In many places, such as Katavi, you will find wilderness that neither progress nor time has spoiled. It is the perfect escape from a hectic life.

When Should You Visit?

There are two seasons in Tanzania – dry and wet. The rains begin towards the end of October and last until January. They return from March to June. In between these rainy spells comes the dry season.

If you travel in the dry season (February, and then July to September), you can climb mountains in safe conditions and explore in lovely sunshine of 77 degrees Fahrenheit (25 degrees Celsius). Driving on the roads is easier, and the animals gather around waterholes, which makes them easier to find.

That said, the rainy season is also a good time to watch large movements of animals. The wildlife thrives on the rain, and the grassland turns lush and colourful. You will take your best photos and see a wide range of animals. It is also the least expensive time to travel and there are fewer crowds to deal with.

Some areas can’t be reached during the rainy season, but the entire northern circuit is usually open all year round.

Do You Need A Visa?

Yes. If you want to visit Tanzania, you need a passport, a return ticket and a single-entry tourist visa. This applies to most countries, but you can check the rules for your own country’s citizens via this link.

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months before the date you arrive, while the visa can be arranged at the airport.

You can leave your visa until the very last moment, but doing so may lead to delays or a refusal. We suggest applying in advance so you avoid higher prices and long queues at immigration.

Duration Of Your Trip

Your safari in Tanzania should ideally last at least 10 days. That gives you enough time to cover the Northern Circuit (Serengeti, Tarangire and Ngorongoro), the most popular safari route in Africa.

If you stay longer, you can add the lakes and the cultural centres of the region as well.

Get Your Vaccinations

Before you travel to Tanzania, it is a good idea to seek advice on the vaccinations below:

  • Yellow Fever
  • Diphtheria
  • Tetanus
  • Hepatitis A
  • Hepatitis B
  • Meningitis
  • Polio
  • Tuberculosis

For stays of more than two weeks, some doctors advise a Typhoid vaccination. And if you will be in direct contact with animals (for example petting them or working on a farm), some doctors suggest adding rabies vaccinations.

Talk to your doctor about the vaccinations you have already had, since many people were protected against diseases such as meningitis as children.

Currencies

Tanzania’s national currency is the Tanzanian Shilling, often shown as TSh or /=. Coins come in 50s, 100s and 200s, while notes come in 500s, 1000s and 5000s.

In most towns you can change US Dollars, UK pounds Sterling and European Euros without any trouble. You may also find that US Dollars and Euros are accepted across large parts of the country.

In July 2022, USD$100 was worth TSh233,200, £100 was worth TSh277,949,91 and €100 was worth TSh234,704.14.

Where To Go And Things To Do

You can do lots in Tanzania, but it all boils down to three things – discovering the local culture, searching for wild animals and exploring the wilderness.

See The Big 5

The Big 5 is a checklist of Africa’s most iconic animals. On a Big 5 safari you look for the African Lion, the African Elephant, the African Leopard, the Rhinoceros and the Cape Buffalo.

Big as these animals are, they can be very hard to find in the wild. Illegal poaching, which reduces their numbers, makes the task harder still. A Big 5 tour guide, however, can take you to the best spots to see these remarkable animals.

Experience The Great Migration

The great migration is the famous journey that thousands of wildebeest make across the plains of Africa. Millions of people have seen it in hundreds of documentaries, yet nothing compares with witnessing this surreal sight in person.

No mammal migration in the world is larger than the wildebeest migration, and you can watch this spectacle in Serengeti National Park.

Climb Mount Kilimanjaro

Mount Kilimanjaro National Park differs from Tanzania’s other national parks. Wildlife is scarce on this tour; the real draw is the climb up Kilimanjaro, Africa’s highest mountain.

Even if you do not make it to the top, the view over the wilderness below is truly heavenly.

Take a safari in Serengeti National Park

Even if the Big 5 or the great migration are not your reason for travelling, Serengeti National Park is still worth a visit. It is Tanzania’s second largest national park, with more than 500 bird species and millions of grazing animals such as gazelles and zebras on open, flat land where they are easy to see.

Travel The Northern Circuit

Tanzania’s best-known national parks lie on the Northern Circuit. As mentioned earlier, you can visit parks such as Serengeti, Tarangire and Ngorongoro. You can also explore Arusha, Lake Manyara, Lake Eyasi, Lake Natron, Kilimanjaro, Olduvai and Mkomazi.

Covering the whole region takes about 10 days, during which you will see animals in their natural surroundings.

If you cannot set aside 10 days for travelling across the endless plains, you can see the whole region far more quickly by leaving out some parts or by using flights on your safari! Don’t let a lack of time stop you from enjoying everything Tanzania has to offer. Picture yourself on a wonderful safari, or gazing over the endless plains from a hot air balloon!

Visit A National Park 

Not close to Tanzania’s northern parks? No need to worry. The country has 35 national parks spread across the north, east, south and west. Each one has its own highlights and attractions, so look for a park near your hotel and plan your wildlife experience from there.

Go To Stone Town

Stone Town is regarded as Tanzania’s architectural capital. Located on the island of Zanzibar, most of its houses date from the 19th century. Their wooden doors are often beautifully carved and carefully looked after.

As the oldest functioning town in the world, Stone Town is a great place for anyone who loves history. Local cultures have stayed the same, so you can see culture and history come alive.

Scuba Dive In Zanzibar

Pemba, a Zanzibar island, is one of Tanzania’s top diving spots. Its white sandy beach is often deserted, and the clear water makes it ideal for scuba diving.

Life here is relaxed, and the island is so hilly that a weekend of swimming followed by mountain biking has become a popular pastime.

Mafia Island Marine Park

Mafia Island has beaches just as beautiful as Pemba’s and the same almost transparent water. On Mafia Island, though, you can also visit the Marine Park, a coral garden lying just off the shore. You can see more than 400 species of bright, colourful fish darting around the coral reef.

The island is also a traditional breeding ground for the endangered green turtle, which makes it one of the few safe havens for this rare species.

Visit Rift Valley

The Rift Valley owes its name to the earth’s weakened crust. It is a geological phenomenon, with the valley splitting the land for thousands of miles. This steep drop has produced natural hot springs, lakes, volcanoes and geysers.

This valley is where flamingos make their home and baboons are in charge. The rift stretches from Syria to Mozambique, and the tectonic plates widen the split a little more every year. In places the top of the rift valley reaches 400 metres, with a very sheer drop. The view from up there is stunning. A favourite spot at the top of the Rift valley is Manyara Best View lodge, a real attraction that is often paired with Tarangire, Ngorongoro or Lake Manyara national park.

Go To A Lake

There are more than 14 large lakes across Tanzania. Lake Chala, on the border between Tanzania and Kenya, has the smallest surface area, at 4.2 square kilometres or 1.6 square miles.

Lake Victoria has the largest surface area, at 68,800 square kilometres, or 26,600 square miles. To put that in perspective, the United Kingdom covers 243,610 square kilometres and Scotland 77,910 square kilometres. So the lake named after Queen Victoria is about as big as one of her countries.

Lake Victoria

It is only the second largest lake in the world, but by a long way it is the largest tropical lake.

The lake is so big that it borders three countries – Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania.

It was given Queen Victoria’s name in 1858 and is home to fish found nowhere else in the world.

Lake Manyara

Lake Manyara is famous above all for its birdlife. More than a million flamingos live here, along with thousands of pelicans, hundreds of storks and even rare species such as the chestnut-banded plover.

Most of the fish are carp, so we suggest this lake to bird lovers rather than fish lovers.

You travel to the lake by jeep, driving through wild forest and shrubland until you reach the marsh. Both the journey and the scenery are breathtaking.

Fishing is permitted on the lake, so you can cast a line while flamingos fly past.

Lake Natron

Natron is an alkaline lake with a pH of 12 or higher. According to scientists, this high alkalinity is caused by lava beneath the water that releases large quantities of carbonate.

Thanks to this unusual alkaline character, the water is home to plants and animals that thrive in salt. Rather than green pigments for photosynthesis, almost all of these flowers use red ones, which makes the lake look as if it is full of blood.

The flamingos feed on the redfish, animals and plants, which gives their feathers a red and pink colour.

Getting here can be very hard because there are hardly any roads, but a professional guide knows the best way to reach it. Lake Natron can’t be visited in the rainy season, however.

Lake Duluti

Rather than a wildlife reserve, this lake is more of a place for sport. You can hike there, paddle a canoe on the water and cycle along the waterways. Swimming is not allowed, but gentle water sports are, so you can enjoy them while taking in the lovely scenery.

Lake Momella

Lake Momella in fact consists of 7 lakes: Big Momela, Small Momelaa, Tulusia, Lekandrio, El Kekhotoito, Rishateni and Kusare.

Each of them is a shallow alkaline lake, ideal for watching flamingos. You are also likely to see pelicans, guinea fowl, African fish eagles, Egyptian geese and African jacanas here.

Because of the high alkalinity, most mammals do not drink the water, but you can still see giraffes, waterbucks and zebras in the area around the lakes.

For the best experience, go on a canoe trip to get around the lakes.

Explore The Beaches Of Zanzibar

If an active holiday appeals to you more than one spent watching animals, we suggest looking for a surf beach on the Zanzibar islands. With white sandy beaches and clear, shallow water, it is easy to learn the sport here.

You can also rent a boat to explore the clear sea while staying near the island’s shore.

Visit Iraqw Villages

The Iraqw tribe is an ancient community that still lives just as it did more than 100 years ago.

More than 150 tribes live and flourish in Tanzania, but the Iraqw tribe gives visitors the chance to share in their way of life. You can chat with the villagers, step inside their homes and take part in their farming.

Visit on the 7th or the 25th of the month and you can see the Iraqw market, where villagers sell everything from cattle to art and from herbs to clothing. It makes for a one-of-a-kind souvenir to bring home.

Visit A Maasai Village

At the Maasai Village, you will find the same authentic culture as in the Iraqw Village. The Maasai, however, still live in the heart of Africa. Lions and elephants roam their land, yet they do not fear these dangerous animals.

Rather, the Maasai know how to live in balance with nature – something they may be able to teach you.

Visit Ngorongoro Crater

We have brought up Ngorongoro National Park several times because it is so special. It is called one of the 7 African wonders, has the highest density of wildlife per square kilometre and offers ideal conditions for animals to live in.

The crater provides a year-round water supply for the animals, so you can watch these lovely creatures gather at the waterhole.

The crater is roughly a million years old and was once among the highest mountains in the world, until a devastating explosion. It was taller than Mt. Kilimanjaro, and this remarkable landscape was formed by the volcanic eruption. Strictly speaking it is a caldera rather than a crater, but it is known as Ngorongoro Crater.

Visit the Arusha Cultural Heritage Center

African art from all over the continent fills the Arusha Cultural Heritage Center. Masks, statues, furniture and crafts are on show, together with the history of tribes both living and long gone.

One gem in particular you will hear about is Tanzanite. It takes its name from Tanzania, its only known source, and is found solely in the Mirerani Hills.

The longing for such a rare and lovely stone sparked wars in countries all over the world. Here you can find out what impact the gem has had on Tanzania and its neighbours.

Visit Olduvai Gorge

You can visit the Olduvai Gorge, a historic archaeological site. The oldest human bones ever found come from this area, among them skulls of around 2 million years old.

Between June and October you may get the chance to speak with an archaeologist working at the site. They can explain their latest finds and what these reveal about our ancestors.

If no archaeologists are at work, you can still chat with the tour guides and visit the museum, where you can buy replicas to take home.

Luxury Accommodations In Tanzania

Would you like a hotel with 5-star service near the animals you will be exploring during the day? Then stay at Singita Faru Faru Lodge or Melia Serengeti Lodge. It lies on the edge of the National Park, giving you gorgeous views as the sun goes down over the plains.

The hotel offers a spa, a private walking safari, a fine dining restaurant and more. You can use the outdoor pool and the hiking trails, with or without a guide.

Prefer to stay close to Tanzania’s cultural hub? Then book Legendary Lodge or Elewana Arusha Coffee Lodge. It sits right in the middle of everything happening in Arusha, yet feels calm and secluded inside.

Since the lodge revolves around coffee, you can taste the best coffee from across the continent, as well as enjoy fine dining and use of the pool and spa.

Finally, if you would like your hotel to be a source of entertainment in its own right, try Tulia Zanzibar, the best romantic luxury resort. With its private beach, scuba diving, shows and fine dining in the evening, you may need reminding that there is more to see beyond its doors.

Luxury Accommodations In Zanzibar

If your Zanzibar holiday is what you want to enjoy most, we have a few more hotels to recommend.

The Residence Zanzibar is a secluded retreat close to the ocean shore. You can choose from 66 luxurious villas, each with cultural comforts from around the globe, so you can pick something from your own country or try something new.

Every room comes with a balcony, ocean views, a private beach and use of the pool.

Baraza Resort and Spa Zanzibar adds a sense of heritage to your holiday. Its architecture shows both Swahili and Arabic influences. The beach is right outside, and the food is heavenly.

Hotel Riu Palace Zanzibar had a renovation not long ago, in 2019. It has pools, whirlpools, a spa and entertainment both during the day and in the evening. The steakhouse serves 5-star food, and you can enjoy your stay with a view over the clear beach.

Tips And Tricks

Before you pack your bags, here are a few tips. Any time you visit a new country, there are cultural and practical differences worth knowing about.

Consider Hiring A Professional Guide

Travelling with us means you have a professional guide with you for your whole stay. A professional guide knows where to find rare wildlife and can take you into the more secure areas.

Without one, you risk missing the finer details and the animals that are hard to find on safari.

Head Off The Beaten Track

At the peak of the dry season, Tanzania can get busy. Once there are more people than animals, it is time to leave the familiar routes. We recommend a visit to Rubondo Island, in the middle of Lake Victoria.

Picture a Robinson Crusoe adventure, with mountains free of people and a wildlife sanctuary carrying on as though humans had never set foot there.

Check Your Airports

Tanzania has several airports, so avoid flying into the capital, Dar es Salaam, only to lose a day getting to your hotel. There are airports just outside Arusha, direct ones to Kilimanjaro and easy connections to Serengeti.

Work out which options save you the most time and money, so you do not waste either by choosing the capital.

Purchase A Local Sim Card

Buy a local SIM card so you are not charged extra for using your phone abroad. Local SIMs give you better wifi and signal, and they cost less because they are designed for use in the country.

We suggest Vodacom, Airtel or Tigo. Airtel is the budget option with the weakest network of the three, but it will still cost less than your foreign SIM. Tigo offers a good connection for a fair price. Vodacom has the strongest network but is more expensive. If your budget allows, a Vodacom SIM card is the one to get.

Always Travel In Groups At Nightime

Tanzania is very welcoming to tourists, but as in any other country there will always be people looking to take advantage of a holidaymaker. To avoid being mugged, move around in groups at night. Criminals can recognise a tourist from far away, so don’t take the risk.

Only Carry Your Daily Basic Essentials 

That brings us to our next tip: carry only what you need for the day. Losing your passport or visa can cause you serious problems when you try to leave the country.

Then again, never leave your hotel room without mosquito spray and sunscreen. Once the heat hits you, these sprays will be your closest companions.

Pack Light

This tip applies more broadly than just to Tanzania. Bring only what you need for hiking, relaxing and swimming at the beach. You can buy extra clothes after you arrive and have them washed at your hotel. Keep the space in your bag for gifts and souvenirs instead.

Packing light also means paying less for your travel. To save as much as possible, buy disposable items once you arrive. Shampoo, travel toothpaste and other small toiletries are sold everywhere near your hotel, so don’t use up luggage space on them.

Remember To Tip

Tipping in Tanzania is as natural as breathing. For everyday interactions, such as someone taking your photo, driving you or serving you in a cafe, you are expected to tip about 10 to 12%. In a fine dining restaurant or a 5-star hotel, however, the expected tip rises to 20%.

A tip is expected for every interaction, so make sure you count out those extra coins before you agree to a purchase.

Summary

The top places to visit in Tanzania are Mount Kilimanjaro, the Northern Circuit safaris, Lake Victoria or Manyara, the Iraqw Village and the Arusha Cultural Heritage Center. Miss all of these on your trip and you will have missed the most iconic parts of the country.

Finally, when you plan your trip, remember to build in some rest days, as all that wonderful wildlife can be overwhelming. So make time for spa days and for relaxing by the sea.

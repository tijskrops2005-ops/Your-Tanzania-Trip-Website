Makuyuni Wildlife Park may not be as famous as some of the big national parks, but the abundance of wildlife together with its vast, secluded setting makes it well worth a visit. The park lies within the Tarangire-Manyara ecosystem, covers 49 square kilometres and sits only 70 kilometres from Arusha, on the road towards Serengeti National Park. It is a beautiful park with striking landscapes, home to lions, elephants, giraffes, zebras and much more, and a rewarding spot for birdwatchers too.

Besides the animals, Makuyuni has remarkably varied vegetation – open savannah, lush woodland and dramatic plains dotted with acacias – which makes a walking safari all the more interesting! Far fewer people visit than in other wildlife areas, so it is a quiet destination for nature lovers who prefer to take in the wild beauty of East Africa at their own pace.

Picture-perfect biodiversity

While you explore the park, the scenery opens up like a painting of golden grasses, towering baobabs and rugged rocky outcrops. The wide, rolling plains form the ideal backdrop for wildlife sightings – and for the photos that go with them! When the sun goes down, the land turns into a sea of warm, glowing colours, giving you that typical, lovely safari atmosphere.

The riverine forests, too, offer shelter to a wide range of wildlife. Driving along the winding tracks you’ll pass seasonal rivers and waterholes that draw all sorts of animals, from elephants to zebras, which adds to the park’s rich biodiversity. It is also a great place to stretch your legs between game drives. Because it’s not an official national park, several hikes are possible here, such as a climb of the 1900-metre-high Kipara Hill. And being a good hour’s drive from Arusha, it may well turn out to be your ideal picnic spot.

Wildlife corridor

Wildlife corridors allow animals to travel between far-apart areas – mostly in search of water and greener pastures – which keeps their gene pool diverse and gives them a range of habitats to live in. The Makuyuni wildlife corridor is a vital link between Tarangire and Manyara National Parks and serves as the main route for the northern sub-population of elephants from Tarangire National Park.

Travel time to Makuyuni Wildlife Park

The park is 70 kilometres from Arusha, near the junction on the highway where the road splits towards Tarangire or Manyara. Expect the drive to take 1 to 1.5 hours, depending on the season and on whether wildlife happens to be crossing as you pass.

Our experts’ travel tip for Makuyuni Wildlife Park

Book a night safari in Makuyuni! Picture the thrill of a lion’s deep, resonant roar rolling through the dark, dense jungle night. Beneath a sky full of stars, surrounded by the mystery of the African wilderness, you might just come face to face with the King of the jungle! As the vehicle creeps quietly along the trails, the tension grows and every rustle in the shadows keeps you on the edge of your seat. After dark, lions behave in ways you seldom see in daylight. Watching these majestic animals patrol their territory, interact with the rest of the pride or even set off on a hunt by moonlight is something you will never forget. And beyond the king himself, you may spot other fascinating nocturnal creatures such as honey badgers, bat-eared foxes, genets, white-tailed mongoose and porcupines.

The best time to visit

Wildlife can be seen in Makuyuni throughout the year, but the dry season (June to October) is regarded as the best period for game viewing. In these months the animals gather around the water sources, which makes them easier to find.

Information and facts

Makuyuni Wildlife Park, on the Tarangire-Manyara wildlife corridor, has plenty of animals but also wonderful mountain views and rolling hills. This hidden gem lets you enjoy both sweeping panoramas and intimate, close-up encounters with nature. Whether you’re after a peaceful escape into the wild or an adventurous safari, the striking landscape of Makuyuni makes it a destination worth your time.

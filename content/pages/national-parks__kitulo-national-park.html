Kitulo National Park is sometimes referred to as the “Garden of God”. With its mountain meadows and forests it ranks among the leading nature reserves in Africa. The Kitulo Plateau lies in the southern highlands of Tanzania, and it was the first national park in tropical Africa set up mainly to protect plant life. For real nature lovers this park alone is reason enough to go on a Tanzania travel.

Flora in the national park

Among botanists the Kitulo Plateau is known as the “Serengeti of Flowers”, as it holds one of the largest concentrations of flowers anywhere in the world. Roughly 400 plant species grow in its montane grasslands. On the plateau you find over 45 species of orchids, along with geophytes, aloes, proteas, geraniums, giant lobelia, lilies, aster daisies and other afro-alpine plants. The mountain meadows are in full bloom during the rainy season from November to April. Many of these species grow only in the Kipengere Mountains and the surrounding highlands, and Brachystelma kituloensis, Impatiens rosulata and Pterygodium Ukingense occur nowhere else but Kitulo National Park.

Kitulo’s orchids

Worldwide there is a big trade in orchids as flowers, yet the danger to Kitulo’s orchids came from elsewhere. Many ethnic groups have a long tradition of digging up orchid bulbs for food. From them they make a dish called Chikanda or Kinaka, something like a meat-free sausage: the tubers are dried after harvesting, then pounded and cooked. What started as a rural delicacy was eaten more and more, in ever greater quantities, and the commercial collecting of orchids rose sharply. This put the orchids of the southern highlands at serious risk, and that is why the plateau was declared a protected national park. Plenty of visitors are also drawn to Kitulo Plateau National Park by its huge wealth of wildflowers, birds and grazing mammals.

Animals in Kitulo Plateau National Park

Apart from a handful of mountain reedbuck and eland, large mammals are fairly scarce in Kitulo. Plants, birds and butterflies more than compensate for that. Native to the park are the rare chimpanzee, blue swallow, red chalk hawk, njembe stalksinger, kipengere seedeater, mountain swamp widow, zebra, kipunji monkey, chameleon, lizards, frogs, spiders and a range of other small insects.

What to do in Kitulo Plateau National Park

A pleasant hike is very much an option here: the park gives you excellent chances to photograph the splendid flora up close and to observe birds from a short distance.

Getting to Kitulo Plateau National Park

The easiest way to reach Kitulo is from Mbeya, a drive of just 1.5 hrs.

From Iringa it takes 5.5 hrs by car.

When to go to Kitulo Plateau National Park

If you can, plan your visit to Kitulo between December and April. For most of Tanzania that is the rainy season, yet for Kitulo Plateau National Park it is exactly the right moment to see the flora at its richest and most varied.

Information and facts

Nicknamed the “Garden of God”, Kitulo National Park ranks among Africa’s foremost nature reserves: a landscape of alpine meadows and woodland on the Kitulo Plateau in the south of Tanzania. Botanists have dubbed the plateau the “Serengeti of Flowers”, since it holds one of the richest collections of flowering plants anywhere on earth.

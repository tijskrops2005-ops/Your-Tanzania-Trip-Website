Set on the floor of the Great Rift Valley, Lake Eyasi is a shallow salt lake with no outflow that fills and shrinks with the seasons. It lies in the north of Tanzania, just below the Ngorongoro highlands. Here there is ample room to slow down, enjoy the quiet and let the scenery sink in. The lake is big and sits at considerable altitude. Whereas the parks further north consist of savannah and dry plains, tall palms line the shores of Lake Eyasi.

Wildlife around the Lake

The big-game populations of the neighbouring parks and conservation areas are absent at Lake Eyasi, so don’t expect the big five here. What the lake does offer is a paradise for birdwatchers, thanks to the many bird species that live here permanently. Between June and November the shallow water supports an enormous variety of breeding waterbirds. The main draw are the big flocks of flamingos and great white pelicans. Along the shores you may also come across African spoonbill, Fischer’s lovebird, grey-headed gulls, pied avocet, spurfowl, stork, barbet, weaver and plenty of other species.

The many hippos at Lake Eyasi are another fine sight. During the day the lake gives them somewhere to escape the fierce sun, and when the water level is high they are known to come and cool down in its brackish water. Various other wildlife species also visit the springs in the surrounding area.

Hadzabe Bushmen living at Lake Eyasi

Around the lake live the Hadzabe bushmen, one of the few remaining tribes whose way of life has barely changed in hundreds of years. Paying them a visit is well worth it. They live entirely off the bush and hunt with bow and arrow. As one of the last hunter-gatherer peoples in Africa, the Hadzabe depend completely on the land, much as our ancestors did in the Stone Age for thousands of years.

Excursions and activities in Lake Eyasi

Alongside the Hadzabe bushmen, the Datoga and Mbulu tribes also live in this area. A visit to the bushmen is worthwhile: they will kindly show you where and how they live and hunt. They draw everything they need from the bush and hunt with bow and arrow. The Hadzabe are among the last hunter-gatherers left in Africa and live purely from the land, in the same way our ancestors did in the Stone Age for thousands of years.

On this cultural activity you get to see the culture, habitat and daily life of the Hadzabe bushmen with your own eyes. Early in the morning you join the bushmen on a hunt around Lake Eyasi, and you find out how they light a fire and prepare their food. In the afternoon there is a visit to a market where the Hadzabe exchange honey, fruit and other goods for the knives, arrows and spears made by the Datoga silversmiths. It is a memorable activity and definitely worth setting aside the extra time for.

Best time to visit Lake Eyasi

You can visit the lake at any time of year. Lake Eyasi has a dry season from June to October, plus two rainy seasons that turn the landscape into a lush expanse of grass. The short rains fall in November and December as scattered showers, while the long rains last from mid-March to May. Heavy rain affects the walking trails and roads, which can make getting around less easy.

Information and facts

Shallow and seasonal, the salt lake of Eyasi sits on the floor of the Great Rift, and hippos come here to cool down in its brackish water. The Hadzabe bushmen who live nearby will take you along on a hunt. Birders will find it a fine spot too, with flamingos, great white pelicans and plenty of other species to spot.

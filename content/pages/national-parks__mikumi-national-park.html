Mikumi National Park is one of the southern national parks in Tanzania. Thanks to its wide horizons and plentiful wildlife, it is often compared to the better-known Serengeti Plains. To the south it borders the Selous Game Reserve, and together the two areas form a single, unusual ecosystem. Here your odds of spotting lions up in the trees are higher than in other parks!

Wildlife in the Mikumi National Park

A wide range of animals live in the park, and most visitors arrive at this National Park hoping to see four of the ‘Big Five’: leopard, lion, elephant and buffalo. It makes a wonderful destination for a Tanzania vacation, and you are sure to enjoy it! One of the highlights is the hippos, which live in a pair of mud pools 5 km north of the main entrance gate, accompanied by an ever-changing cast of water-birds.

Within Mikumi National Park, the broad Mkata Foodplain is an excellent area for watching wildlife. From the flattened tops of termite mounds, or in the rainy season sometimes from perches high in the trees, lions keep watch over their grassy realm and the herds of zebra, wildebeest, impala and buffalo that migrate across it. Giraffes browse in the scattered acacia stands along the Mkata River, pockets of shade that Mikumi’s elephants also favour. The Mkata Foodplain is possibly the most dependable spot in Tanzania to see the powerful eland, the largest antelope on earth. The no less impressive greater kudu and sable antelope roam the miombo-covered foothills of the mountains rising along the borders of Mikumi National Park.

Birds in Mikumi

With more than 400 different bird species recorded, Mikumi is also a fine destination for bird lovers!

Along the Mkata River you can enjoy majestic views of Open-billed Storks fishing. Birds commonly seen in Mikumi National Park include the Marabou stork, Red-billed oxpeckers, Black-bellied bustard, Francolin, Cattle egret, Guinea-fowls, Hornbill and Malachite Kingfischer. The most colourful of all are the Yellow-throated long claw, the Lilac-breasted roller and the Bateleur eagle. In the rainy season, European migrants are frequently spotted in Mikumi National Park.

Expert travel tips for Mikumi National Park

I am Aaron, and I have guided in Mikumi National Park for 10 years. Four of the Big Five can be seen in Mikumi, though they take a little more effort to find than in the northern parks. The hippos and the birdlife leave the strongest impression on most of our guests.

How long to stay? 1 or 2 days

A few tips on the best time to go and how to get there:

  • Stay away in the wet months; my advice is to visit between June and October, since the park cannot be reached during the wet season (March to May).
  • Schedule this park at the start of your trip, so the journey builds up in the right order: Selous and Ruaha are without doubt the highlights.
  • Do not combine Mikumi with Selous by road. The drive is far too long (a full day on fairly uncomfortable roads); take a plane for this leg instead.

Which hotel?

Compared with other national parks, the hotels in Mikumi are fairly basic. Bearing that in mind, my recommendations would be:

  • Camp Bastian (Silver)
  • Vuma Hills (Golden)
  • Stanley’s Kopje Camp (Platinum)

How to get to Mikumi

Now that the paved road between Dar es Salaam and the park gate is complete, Mikumi National Park is easy to reach for safaris: the drive from Dar Es Salaam takes six to seven hours.

Mikumi National Park shares borders with Udzungwa National Park and Ruaha National Park. Together, the three destinations make up a varied and enjoyable safari circuit.

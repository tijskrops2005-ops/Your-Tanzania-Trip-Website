Ngorongoro Conservation Area

Welcome to Africa's Leading Tourist Attraction 2025: the Ngorongoro Crater. Besides the highest concentration of wildlife in Africa, including the Big Five and the entire cast of The Lion King, this one-of-a-kind scenery is also home to the famous Maasai tribe.

Stretching from Serengeti National Park in the north to the Great Rift Valley in the east, the Ngorongoro Conservation Area covers over 8,000 km² (3,200 sq. miles) altogether. Within it lie the Ngorongoro Crater, Ndutu, Olduvai Gorge, Empakaai, Olmoti Crater and Oldonyo Lengai Mountain. Its blend of forest, valleys, savanna, craters, lakes and swamps supports a broad range of wildlife.

Natural wonder Ngorongoro Crater

At the heart of the area sits the well-known Ngorongoro Crater. Some two to three million years ago a huge volcano erupted and then caved in on itself. This formed the crater, and with it a one-of-a-kind ecosystem holding many different types of vegetation.

The crater now holds the highest concentration of wildlife in Africa. Zebras, buffaloes, warthogs, baboons, elephants, flamingos, ostriches and hippos all live here. Predators are plentiful too, among them lions, hyenas, jackals, cheetahs and leopards. Naturally, the Big Five – buffalo, elephant, leopard, lion, rhino – are a major draw of the Ngorongoro Crater, and every one of them can be spotted inside this crater, which measures 20 kilometres (12.5 miles) across and 600 metres (2,000 feet) in depth. Take in the range of animals and the lovely colours of the scenery. Looking down from the crater rim onto the floor below is an incredible sight. Thanks to its natural beauty, the crater has been named one of Africa’s Seven Natural Wonders.

Maasai tribe

Information and facts

The main draw of this area is the Ngorongoro Crater, which holds the highest concentration of wildlife in Africa. Lions, elephants, zebras, black rhino and much more can be seen here. Looking out from the crater rim over the floor is spectacular, so it is no surprise that it was named one of Africa’s Seven Natural Wonders.

Kilimanjaro National Park draws travellers who are ready for a challenge. On this page we go through everything you need to know about trekking Mount Kilimanjaro, from the Kilimanjaro itineraries we recommend most to the practical side of getting ready for the adventure of a lifetime.

Kilimanjaro itineraries

Once you have decided to climb Mount Kilimanjaro, your next task is picking the route that suits you best. Seven main Mount Kilimanjaro routes lead to the summit in total. One of them starts on the northern side of the mountain; the other six start on the southern side. Your Tanzania Trip offers Kilimanjaro climbing itineraries on every one of these routes. So the big question is: which route is right for you?

Kilimanjaro routes: pros and cons

You can pick from seven different Kilimanjaro climbing routes, each with its own advantages and drawbacks to match the ability of the hiker. They vary in length, difficulty, scenery, crowds, accommodation and a whole range of other factors.

The team at Your Tanzania Trip knows how to pinpoint the best route for your trip, taking all the stress out of your once-in-a-lifetime African adventure. Having guided clients from all over the world to hundreds of successful summits, we take the time to listen to the specific needs and wishes of your group when we organise your Kilimanjaro climb. Among our clients, the Machame Route, the Maranga Route and the Lemosho Route are the most popular choices.

Machame Route

Kilimanjaro’s Machame Route, nicknamed the “Whiskey Route”, is one of the most popular climbing routes in Tanzania, and it has an impressively high success rate too. The ascent is gradual, giving your body more time to adapt to the altitude. The trail winds around the mountain, serving up spectacular views and a great variety of landscapes.

Along this route you walk through green rainforest up to the remarkable Shira Plateau. The final push to the crater rim at Stella Point is probably the toughest section of the route, and it does call for at least a basic level of fitness.

You decide how long the Machame Route takes! It can be completed in 6 or 7 days, and in our experience the odds of reaching the top of Africa’s largest mountain are far better on a 7-day Kilimanjaro hike!

PRO: Because the route is shorter and easy to reach, the Machame Route costs less than most of the others

CON: The route can get fairly crowded, particularly in peak season

Marangu Route

Nicknamed the “Coca-Cola” route, this trail is known as one of the easiest ways up the mountain. It is certainly the shortest route, but that does not automatically make it the easiest! With less time on the mountain, your body has less chance to acclimatise properly, which raises the risk of fatigue and ultimately lowers your chances of making it to the summit on this route.

Some climbers choose this route because you sleep in huts rather than in tents, as you do on most other mountain treks. The flip side is less privacy! (And there is nothing quite like camping beneath African skies!) The Marangu Route is the best option if you are watching your budget, as it is one of the cheapest ways to reach the top of Kilimanjaro.

PRO: The Marangu Route is among the cheapest and fastest routes up Mount Kilimanjaro. It also provides hut accommodation in dormitories, a plus for anyone who would rather not camp!

CON: The route can get fairly crowded, particularly in peak season. Because you descend along the same trail you climbed, you see less of the spectacular scenery!

Lemosho Route

The Lemosho Route is a beautiful and distinctive route, famous for its breathtaking views. Unlike the other Mount Kilimanjaro climbing routes, it begins on the western flank of Kilimanjaro. As a result, the Leomosho Route sees fewer climbers and gives you a far more secluded hiking experience.

What is more, the Lemosho trail is entirely unspoilt, making the climb to the famous Shira Plateau a stunningly beautiful adventure. After a few days the Lemosho Route merges with the Machame Route at Lava Tower. The route takes 7 or 8 days and does demand a reasonable level of fitness!

CONS: Lemosho is without doubt a pricier route: the trail is long and you need extra transport to reach the far side of the mountain.

PROS: It is money well spent! On the Lemosho Route climbers get plenty of time to acclimatise properly, the views are spectacular and the summit success rates are high. It is one of the most beautiful and complete ways to climb Kilimanjaro.

Best time to climb Kilimanjaro

You can climb Mount Kilimanjaro at any time of year. At Your Tanzania Trip we give expert advice on the best moment to plan your Kilimanjaro trip, based on your personal requirements.

The two main climbing seasons are the dry months of December to March and June to October. December to March is the warmest period: the skies are clearer and snow on the trail is less likely. February is the best month of all, with high temperatures and a low chance of rain. From June to October it is noticeably colder on the mountain and the trails are busier.

In the rainy season conditions can be wet and slippery, and visibility may be poor. Most climbers steer clear of these months, although some experienced climbers and adventurous travellers actually prefer to go then. There are definite upsides, and the absence of crowds is a big one!

How to prepare for climbing Kilimanjaro

Although Mount Kilimanjaro has a route for every level, you need to be able to handle roughly 6-7 hours of walking every single day. That can be exhausting, so preparing is always a good idea. On a typical climbing day you will be on your feet for 6-7 hours. In the months before your trip, go on long hikes every few weeks, slowly building your stamina until you can manage a full weekend of hiking.

Yoga and breathing classes are other useful ways to prepare for your trek. Stretching body and mind makes your muscles stronger and less likely to fail you when you need them most!

Climbing Kilimanjaro costs

What it costs to climb Kilimanjaro depends on the route you pick. We work together with our clients to build custom Kilimanjaro itineraries for all kinds of budgets, from shoestring to bespoke luxury. Beyond the tour operator fee, other costs to keep in mind are:

  • Getting the right gear: walking poles, hiking boots, a waterproof daypack, breathable clothing and so on.
  • Travel insurance that covers a Kilimanjaro climb.
  • Tips for guides and porters: they work extremely hard, often for little pay. Tipping is standard practice on Kilimanjaro and helps support the local community.

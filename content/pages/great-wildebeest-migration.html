Quick facts about the Great Wildebeest Migration

  • With close to two million animals moving across the plains of Tanzania every year, the Great Wildebeest Migration ranks among the biggest land mammal migrations on earth.
  • Wildebeest give the migration its name and make up most of the animals, but zebras, topi, gazelles and other species travel with the herds too.
  • As a rule the migration kicks off in December and continues through to November, though exactly when it starts hinges on the arrival of Tanzania’s rainy season.
  • Over a full migration cycle the herds travel roughly 1,000 kilometres.
  • As many as 250,000 wildebeest fall victim to predators in the course of the migration.
  • On the move, the wildebeest split up into herds of hundreds of thousands of animals.

Where and when does the Great Wildebeest Migration happen?

Here is the catch: the precise timing of the Serengeti wildebeest migration follows the rainfall patterns of each year. That makes forecasting where and when it begins tricky. Even the wildebeest don’t know!

Because of climate change the rainy seasons no longer arrive on a regular schedule, nor are they easy to predict. That is also why the migration has slipped out of sync in recent years.

A MONTH-BY-MONTH BREAKDOWN

December to April

Usually the migration gets under way in late November or early December, when the moving herds reach the southern part of Tanzania’s Serengeti National Park around Ndutu.

Once in the Serengeti, the animals roam wherever they like. It is a truly majestic sight: almost two million wildebeest and zebras spread out across the Tanzanian plains, feeding on fresh, nutritious grass.

The calving season starts in February, when some half a million calves arrive on these southern plains. Predators are plentiful then too, all hoping for an easy meal.

Come April the herds set off for the central and western Serengeti to find fresh, long grass. With the arrival of the rains, the mating season begins.

May to June

In May and June the wildebeest and zebra herds push further north, preparing for the perilous Grumeti River crossing, where huge crocodiles lie in wait.

It is a frightening thing to watch: thousands of these magnificent animals battling the forces of nature and predators. Sadly, not every animal makes it across.

Those that survive carry on, some herds passing through the Grumeti Game Reserve and Ikorongo Game Reserve, others taking the route straight through the heart of Serengeti National Park.

July to September

Between July and September the herds gather around the Mara River, which they must cross to reach Kenya. The scenes at this time are as dramatic as they are impressive: the fast current and the crocodiles make this crossing extremely dangerous for the herds.

The Mara River crossing is the toughest obstacle of the whole migration. It produces a spectacular and often hair-raising scene of panic and chaos as herds of hundreds of thousands of animals attempt to get through the raging water.

Interestingly, during the past two years nearly two-thirds of the migration stayed put instead of crossing the Mara River, because the northern Serengeti still had enough fresh grass.

October to December

In October and November the wildebeest migration leaves the northern Serengeti and heads back south towards Ndutu through the central Seronera region. The herds stay there until late November/early December, when the yearly cycle begins again.

When is the best time to see the Great Wildebeest Migration in Tanzania?

There is no simple answer to when the Great Wildebeest Migration is best experienced; it depends entirely on what you hope to see. And with the rainy seasons having become so unpredictable, naming an exact date is extremely hard.

Broadly speaking, the migration can be split into five events or seasons:

Approximate Period Event Place
December to January Beginning of the migration Southern Serengeti
February to April Calving & breeding season Southern Serengeti
May to June Grumeti River crossing Western Serengeti
July to September Mara River crossing Masai Mara and Northern Serengeti
October to November The trek south Masai Mara and Northern Serengeti to Southern Serengeti

All things considered, most travellers hoping to see the migration schedule their safari between July and September. The main reason is the Mara River crossing, by far the most thrilling and spectacular moment of the Great Wildebeest Migration.

Where is the best place to see the Great Wildebeest Migration?

Where to go to watch the wildebeest migration, just like when, comes down to what you hope to see. From December to April, for instance, the Southern and Central Serengeti is where you should look for a place to stay.

That is when the herds spread across the plains of the Southern Serengeti and remain there for the calving and breeding season. Calving season draws many tourists keen to witness the dance of life and death, as predators try to make easy prey of the young calves.

If the mighty river crossings are what you want to see, however, they take place in the northern parts of Serengeti and in the Masai Mara region (Kenya).

Several tented camps sit in the key areas, ideally placed for seeing the Great Wildebeest Migration in Tanzania in all its glory.

Below are a few tips for planning your wildebeest migration safari.

Book Early

A safari is not something to plan and book at the last minute. That goes for any safari, but it matters most during the peak wildebeest migration season from June to October.

That is when tourists pour into Tanzania to see the migration, and camps and lodges fill up quickly. So if the river crossings are on your list, book early, ideally at least a year ahead.

Know When to Go

As mentioned, Tanzania is busy with tourists in peak season, above all in Serengeti National Park, where most of the migration unfolds. If you would rather avoid the crowds, consider another time of year and experience the migration on your own terms, without hordes of tourists competing for the best spots.

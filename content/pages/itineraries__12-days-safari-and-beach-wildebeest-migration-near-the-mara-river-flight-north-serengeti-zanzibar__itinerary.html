Day 2

Tarangire National Park

Silver
Gold
Platinum

Tarangire National Park is famous for its large herds of elephants and its huge number of giant baobab trees. These trees can grow hundreds of years old, and in the dry season they even provide water for the elephants, which use a special technique to get water out of them. On top of that, the Tarangire River is the only water source during the dry season, so at that time of year you can see herds of up to 250 elephants in Tarangire, along with many other animals!

Day 3

Ngorongoro Conservation Area (incl. Crater)

Silver
Gold
Platinum

The well-known Ngorongoro Crater counts as one of Africa's seven natural wonders. It came into being two million years ago, when a huge volcano erupted. The fertile lava gave rise to a unique ecosystem that now has the highest density of animals anywhere in Africa! Inside the crater you are surrounded by thousands of animals, including lions, zebras, buffalos and black rhinos, and the views are amazing – you won’t know which way to look!

Day 4

Serengeti National Park (central west/east)

Silver
Gold
Platinum

Serengeti National Park is known far and wide as the place for a safari, largely thanks to the Great Migration that moves through it. The huge, thundering herds are impressive, and so is the number of predators living here: more than four thousand lions, more than 200 cheetahs, a thousand leopards, 3500 hyenas and hundreds of wild dogs! Plus, of course, many other beautiful animals that your guide will be happy to look for with you. Are you ready?

Day 5 - 6

Northern Serengeti & the Great Migration (possibility to see the famous Mara River crossing)

Silver
Gold
Platinum

Picture yourself driving across the grassy plains of the northern Serengeti with your guide and finding millions of wildebeests and zebras, groaning and bleating while they try to cross a river teeming with crocodiles. You are watching the famous Mara River crossing of the Great Migration, a natural wonder you can see every year from July to October in the north of Serengeti National Park. It is a tense spectacle to watch - after all, have we mentioned the big cats on the far bank, every bit as hungry for meat as the herds are for fresh grazing...?

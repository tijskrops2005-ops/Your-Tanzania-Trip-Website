Because it lies close to several of the best-known game reserves in the country, Arusha is a favourite base for safaris in Tanzania. Yet the city is more than a place you pass through; it has plenty to offer in its own right. Think thrilling hikes, canoe trips and colourful markets – Arusha is full of exciting things to see and do. In this brief article we have gathered the attractions we love most. All that is left for you is to choose your favourite(s) and set off. Our pleasure.

Things to Do in Arusha, Tanzania

Walk to Miriakamba, the first camp on Mount Meru

Mount Meru may stand in the shadow of its big brother, Mount Kilimanjaro, but it deserves your attention, with a quiet beauty of its own and rewarding challenges for keen hikers.

The trail to Miriakamba Camp is a true fairytale for anyone who loves nature. At one moment you are wandering through mossy forest, at the next you emerge into clearings that give you glimpses of the summit of Meru.

Then there is the wildlife. Rather than an ordinary walk in the park, it feels as if you’re inside an episode of your favourite nature documentary. Playful monkeys swing through the treetops, and now and then a majestic buffalo eyes you sideways – animals like these bring an exciting touch of unpredictability to your hike.

Hike to the Mount Meru Waterfall

The walk to the Mount Meru Waterfall (also called Napuru Waterfall) is, quite simply, a dream for anyone who loves the outdoors. Lasting roughly an hour, the trail takes you through lush green rainforest, where you will be welcomed by cheeky monkeys. Stay alert: you may also see zebras in the distance, several kinds of antelope grazing, or a solitary buffalo watching you with mild curiosity.

The waterfall is the high point of the tour. Seeing the water plunge around 24 metres (80 feet) into a crystal-clear pool will leave you gasping for that wonderfully fresh air. Like a cool(ing) shower? Go on, you have certainly earned it!

Should you choose to eat outdoors with a packed picnic, you won't find a finer setting than these majestic falls – just breathe, unwind, eat and do it all again.

Go on a walking safari in Arusha National Park

On this rather special safari, there is no engine noise, only the sounds of nature. With a guide at your side you walk through the park and come close to zebras, giraffes, several types of antelope and the well-known black-and-white colobus monkeys of the park. A nice bonus: your guide also shares background on the local plants and fun facts about the animals.

In the park you will also find the beautiful Ngurdoto Crater, which people often call the “Little Ngorongoro” – thanks to its lush green floor, it is a real refuge for all kinds of creatures with four legs or wings. The Momela Lakes are another highlight, with a calm setting that stands out against the wild scenery around them. Do you love birds – or the colour pink? Then this is the place for you, as huge numbers of flamingos live on these lakes.

A word of warning: Encounters with wild animals look much smoother on screen than in reality, and nobody has the leading role in “I Petted a Lion (and Lived to Regret It)” on their bucket list 😉 So please do exactly what your guide tells you. Thank you.

Go Canoeing in Arusha National Park

Let’s linger in Arusha National Park a little longer, as it really is so lovely! Why not give your trip a dash of adventure by joining a canoe tour? Out on the calm water of the Momela Lakes, you can just paddle your cares away. The noise of the city will feel a world away while you enjoy the peaceful views across the lake, the lively wildlife and the fresh mountain air.

Climb Mount Longido

Just outside Arusha rises Mount Longido, a striking volcanic peak of 2,636 metres (8,651 ft) with superb panoramic views over the landscape around it. If you want a refreshing day hike close to Arusha, climbing Longido is an excellent choice. The climb up Longido takes most walkers about six hours. At first the path winds through lush forest, then it gives way to rockier ground. The higher you get, the wider the views over Mount Meru, the Namanga Hills, Ol Doinyo Lengai and, on a particularly clear day, even Kilimanjaro.

Pro tip: this is not an easy mountain to climb, so bring plenty of determination along with your kit.

Tanzanite Museum 

If you are nearby, make sure you don't skip the Tanzanite Museum in Arusha, a one-of-a-kind attraction. Its remarkable collection of tanzanite gems will make your eyes light up. Discover the story of this rare precious stone and admire some of the finest examples in the world from close by. Fallen for the sparkling stones? Then just buy a tanzanite jewellery piece of your own in the museum gift shop.

Go mountain biking in and around Arusha

Explore Arusha and its surroundings on two wheels rather than four – and work off a few calories on the way! By mountain bike you can discover the varied terrain of the region, with stunning views and routes for every level of rider.

Some hotspots include:

  • Mount Meru, with forest trails and wildlife sightings, suits intermediate and advanced riders.
  • Arusha National Park combines natural dirt tracks with forest trails, making it perfect for wildlife lovers of every cycling level.
  • The cultural Usa River Trails take you through coffee plantations and local villages.
  • Lake Duluti’s loop trail suits novices and birdwatchers equally well.

Visit a Coffee Plantation in Arusha

Were you aware that Tanzania is known for growing some of the finest coffee beans on earth? It really is, which is why a visit to the coffee fields of Arusha should be on your list. Finding out how coffee is cultivated, picked and processed in Tanzania makes this outing both informative and enjoyable. As you take in the beauty of the rolling hills lined with tidy rows of coffee plants, your well-informed guide shares all he knows about growing coffee. Naturally, the day finishes with a cup of freshly brewed coffee – enjoy!

Visit the Meserani Snake Park and Maasai Museum

The Meserani Snake Park makes an enjoyable family outing, with informative shows for children… and adults. You will find many kinds of snakes there, including puff adders, green and black mambas and the Egyptian cobra, plus crocodiles, lizards and, strangely, birds.

At the cultural Boma on site is the Maasai Museum, where traditional artefacts are on display. It teaches you about the customs and culture of the semi-nomadic Maasai people. Through traditional weapons, beadwork, gourds and everyday items, you get a close look at how these fascinating indigenous people live. It is a wonderful cultural experience that you should not miss.

Take a City Tour

Arusha has lots of interesting sights and historic landmarks that are well worth discovering on a guided tour of the city. A half-day or full-day city tour lets you see the highlights of Arusha in little time. Best of all, you need not think about directions or the best route. Simply take in the atmosphere, the stories and the views. That's it.

Buy a Souvenir At Arusha Maasai Market

If you want a souvenir for family and friends – or for yourself – the Arusha Maasai Market is a well-known place to go. Close to the clock tower in the centre of Arusha, the market is open every day of the week and gathers artisans and craftspeople who sell traditional Maasai goods. Haggling is welcome here, and in fact expected. Feel free to negotiate the price of anything you like. Bargain with good humour and a smile, and you will probably get the best price.

Among the highlights of a shopping trip to the Maasai Market are:

  • Colourful Maasai beadwork jewellery, which often uses colours and symbols with cultural meaning.
  • Carvings of wild animals and Maasai warriors, made from local wood such as ebony.
  • Shukas and other loosely woven, hand-dyed fabrics in bright colours.

Getting Around Arusha

Half the fun lies in getting around this lively city and the area around it. With so much beautiful scenery, the journey between each adventure becomes a sightseeing trip of its own.

By Bus or Public Transport

Catch a glimpse of everyday life and culture by jumping on one of the colourful (and usually packed) buses or shared minibuses of Arusha, known as dala dalas. The routes and timetables may baffle first-timers, but tickets cost very little and you can chat with friendly locals – what could be more authentic?

Self Drive

Nothing compares with the freedom of your own 4×4 in Arusha. Treat yourself to a hire car or 4WD vehicle so you can travel on your own timetable. Behind the wheel yourself, you can head to more remote places and find hidden gems at your own speed. Just be prepared for rough, unsealed roads and make sure you have some basic car repair skills, such as changing a tyre.

Join an Organized Tour

Arusha has a huge choice of tours, from budget day trips to luxury tailor-made safaris. Travelling in a small group makes things easy and safe, and you benefit from the knowledge of experienced guides. The experts take care of the details, so you can enjoy the magic.

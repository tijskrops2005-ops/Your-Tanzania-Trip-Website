Set in Kenya’s Great Rift Valley, the Masai Mara National Reserve is made up largely of open grassland. It shares a border with Serengeti National Park, and between them the two host Africa’s most famous natural wonder: the annual great migration. The reserve was named Masai Mara as a tribute to the Maasai people.

Meet the Maasai tribe in the Masai Mara

The Maasai have lived here ever since they moved into the area from the Nile Basin long ago. In Maa, the language of the Maasai, Mara means ‘spotted’, a reference to the many short, bushy trees scattered across the savannah plains that you notice while driving through the park. If you would like to see how the Maasai live, you can visit one of their villages in the Masai Mara.

Great migration

More than 95 species of animal and over 400 bird species live in the National Reserve, with the wildlife generally found around the reserve’s escarpment. From August until mid October you can come to the Masai Mara for the great migration of wildebeest, zebra, gazelle, antelope and impala. By then the herds have most likely crossed over from Tanzania’s Serengeti National Park on the far side of the border. Over 2.5 million animals take part in this yearly wildebeest migration. In herds of thousands they brave the dangerous Mara River to reach fresh grass on the opposite bank, while crocodiles lie in wait in the water. It is a thrilling spectacle that you really shouldn’t miss!

Masai Mara – Mara Triangle region

People travel to the Masai Mara Triangle because it ranks among the finest wildlife-spotting destinations on earth. The Mara River flows through the Mara Triangle. This is the first area the great migration arrives at when it moves from the Serengeti into the Masai Mara, so your chances of seeing large numbers of animals here are excellent. The famous Big Five (elephant, lion, leopard, rhino and buffalo) all live in the Masai Mara, and with some luck you may also see cheetahs, servals, hyenas, bat-eared foxes, and black-backed and side-striped jackals. Hippos, crocodiles, baboons, warthogs, topis, elands, Thomson’s gazelles, Grant’s gazelles, impalas, waterbucks, oribis, reed-bucks, zebras and many other animals are found in the Mara Triangle as well.

How to get there

From Nairobi, Kenya’s capital, it is a 45-minute flight to the Masai Mara. A number of airstrips serve the various parts of the reserve.

Getting to the Masai Mara from Tanzania is not straightforward, as there is no customs office in the vicinity.

Climate

During the dry season, from June to November and again in February and March, daytime temperatures can climb to 30°C. In the rainy season from November through May, with the heaviest rain in December-January and April-May, temperatures frequently fall to 20°C. Rain rarely lasts all day: mornings are usually sunny, with clouds gathering towards the afternoon and evening.

Best time to visit Masai Mara

Our recommendation is to visit the Masai Mara between August and mid October, while the great migration is there. That is the only period when it offers something extra compared with Serengeti National Park in Tanzania.

Information and facts

The Masai Mara National Reserve lies in Kenya’s Great Rift Valley. Its reputation as a top destination rests on the annual wildebeest migration, which passes through here with the famous crossings of the Mara River. The best period to visit the Masai Mara is from August to mid October, when the migration arrives in Kenya and the river crossings happen. The Serengeti is equally worth visiting at that time, and indeed in the other months of the year, since the migration can be followed in different parts of the Serengeti all year round. In the Masai Mara you stand a good chance of seeing the Big Five along with cheetahs, hyenas, hippos, crocodiles and many other animals. Compared with the Serengeti, the Masai Mara is somewhat busier with tourists.

Facts about Masai Mara

No two years are alike and nothing about the movement of the great migration is guaranteed, but a general pattern can be observed:

  • The great migration occurs in both the Masai Mara and the Serengeti
  • In July still on the Serengeti side
  • Between August and mid September the herds are split, with half in the Serengeti and the other half in the Masai Mara
  • From mid September to mid October more are on the Masai Mara side (the best time to visit the Masai Mara)
  • If you want to watch the river crossing, the Serengeti is less busy; most people regard it as the prime area

Best period to visit the Masai Mara: mid September to mid October

Would you like to experience Mount Kilimanjaro without the climb? Then the walk from Materuni to the Kuringe waterfalls suits you perfectly! A local guide sets off with you from Materuni, and after 40 minutes of lovely walking you reach the impressive waterfall, 70 meters high. Your first real game drive takes place today, in Arusha National Park! Close to Arusha, this park is famous for its many tropical birds, the black and white Colobus monkey and a few rare albino baboons. Day 4 - 5

Tarangire National Park

Silver
Gold
Platinum

Tarangire National Park is famous for its large elephant herds and its great number of giant baobab trees. These trees can reach an age of hundreds of years, and in the dry season elephants actually use them as a water source, thanks to a special technique for getting the water out. On top of that, the Tarangire River is the only water source during the dry season, so at that time of year you can see herds of up to 250 elephants in Tarangire, alongside many other animals!

Lake Manyara National Park is located right on the 400 meters deep Great Rift Valley, between the Ngorongoro Crater and Tarangire National Park. Many different animals are drawn to its wide variety of landscapes. You could visit the lake, for instance, which covers 75 percent of the park, cross a thick rainforest where hundreds of monkeys and birds live, or watch elephants from close by in the woodlands.

Day 8 - 9

Lake Natron

Silver
Gold
Platinum

In the north of Tanzania, just above the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, you find Lake Natron, a place unlike any other. The alkaline lake is famous for its striking red colour and is a paradise for photographers. That is even more true from August to October, when thousands of flamingos turn the lake an even deeper pink. The shimmering heat over the dry, dusty land, the mirror-like water and the volcanic mountains all around create a raw, surreal beauty you are unlikely to forget!

Serengeti National Park has a worldwide reputation as the place for a safari, largely thanks to the Great Migration moving through it. The huge herds on the run are impressive, and so is the number of predators living here: more than four thousand lions, more than 200 cheetahs, a thousand leopards, 3500 hyenas and hundreds of wild dogs! Day 13

Ngorongoro Conservation Area (incl. Crater)

Silver
Gold
Platinum

The well-known Ngorongoro Crater counts as one of Africa's seven natural wonders. It came into being two million years ago, when a huge volcano erupted. The fertile lava gave rise to a unique ecosystem, which now holds the highest concentration of animals anywhere in Africa! Inside the crater you are surrounded by thousands of animals, including lions, zebras, buffalos and black rhinos, and the views are stunning – you won’t know which way to look!

Day 14

Empakaai Crater hike

Silver

Around 40 kilometers northeast of its famous big brother, the Ngorongoro Crater, lies the Empakaai Crater, in a spot that feels almost like the end of the world. You walk from the rim down to the lake on the crater floor, which takes roughly 45 minutes. A pink glow greets you, since the lake is often covered with flocks of flamingos. The views from the rim are stunning: on clear days you can even make out the majestic Ol Donyo Lengai.

Day 15

Visit Lake Eyasi and the Hadzabe tribe

Silver
Gold
Platinum

A day trip to Lake Eyasi and the Hadzabe tribe who live there is a fascinating experience. South of the Ngorongoro Highlands, Lake Eyasi is a shallow, seasonal salt lake and a paradise for bird lovers. Look for hippos in the shallows, then get your binoculars out to search for the African spoonbill, flamingos, gray headed gulls, great white pelicans and pied avocets, among others. Afterwards you visit the Hadzabe bushmen, who are masters of bow hunting.

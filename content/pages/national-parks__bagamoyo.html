Bagamoyo was established towards the close of the 18th century as an outgrowth of Kaole, a far older settlement dating back to the 8th century. For a while the town even served as the capital of German East Africa, and it ranked among the leading harbours for commerce along the coast of East Africa.

Bagamoyo’s History

The Kaole ruins contain what is left of two mosques and 30 graves dating from the 13th century. Around the 17th century a settlement started to develop 2-3 miles to the north of Kaole, and in the 18th century this area became prosperous. As a key stop on the caravan trade routes it received the name Bagamoyo (meaning “relief and rest”). Until the 18th century it remained a small trading centre whose inhabitants were mostly fishermen and farmers.

Trade industry

Fish, salt and rubber were the chief commodities. In the first half of the 19th century the town became a harbour for ivory and for the slave trade, with merchants arriving from the African interior en route to Zanzibar. That history lies behind the meaning of Bagamoyo (“Bwaga-Moyo”), which in Kiswahili translates as “lay down your heart”. One explanation links the name to the slaves marched through the town (“give up all hope”); another to the porters who rested here after hauling 35 pounds of goods on their shoulders from the Great Lakes region (“unload and rest”).

Numerous European explorers, among them Richard Burton, Henry Morton Stanley and David Livingstone, started and finished their expeditions in Bagamoyo. In 1868 French missionaries set up Freedom Village here as a refuge for freed slaves, and for the remainder of the century the town was a stopover for missionaries heading inland from Zanzibar. Once the German Empire decided in 1905 to lay a railway from Dar Es Salam into the interior, Bagamoyo’s significance started to fade. Today the town is a centre for building dhow sailing boats.

The government is making efforts to protect the colonial ruins in and around Bagamoyo and to bring the town back to life. With its relaxed pace and rich history, Bagamoyo makes for an enjoyable day trip or weekend away.

Travel time to Bagamoyo

Dar Es Salaam – Bagamoyo: 2 hrs by car

By car from Saadani National Park to Bagamoyo: 4.5 hrs

Best time to visit Bagamoyo

June through October is the best period for a visit, as this is the dry season and there is hardly any rain. The skies are clear then and the sea is a striking sapphire blue set against azure. The highlight of the year is the Bagamoyo Arts Festival, which normally takes place in late September or October.

Information and facts

The old settlement of Bagamoyo was at one time a leading harbour for commerce along the coast of East Africa. Early on, fish, salt and rubber changed hands here; during the first half of the 19th century ivory and slaves followed. Close by, the Kaole ruins preserve what is left of two mosques and 30 graves dating back to the 13th century.

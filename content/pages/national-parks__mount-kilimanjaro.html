Mount Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa and the tallest free-standing mountain anywhere on earth. Every year, thousands of climbers and adventurers from across the globe are drawn to it. Climbing Mount Kilimanjaro is a bit like walking from the tropics to the arctic in a single week. The altitude and the speed of the ascent make it a genuine challenge, but one that is hugely rewarding.

UNESCO added Mount Kilimanjaro to its World Heritage list in 1987 on account of its natural beauty. Thanks to the mountain's position and enormous height, the range of vegetation and scenery is remarkable: from tropical rainforest full of monkeys and birds up to the ‘Roof of Africa’, with its glaciers and an impressive snow-capped summit. On a classic climb you cross one vegetation zone roughly every day and a half.

Reaching the top of Mount Kilimanjaro after days of walking is an incredible thrill. At the summit your reward is a breathtaking view over the African savannah and the Rift Valley. Climbing Mount Kilimanjaro really is a once-in-a-lifetime experience!

Anyone can climb the highest mountain in Africa!

Is climbing Mount Kilimanjaro a dream of yours, but you are not sure you can manage it? It is certainly demanding, yet you need no climbing experience to reach the summit. If you are healthy and fit, it is well within your reach. Our experienced, professional team will get you to the top safely.

That is why 95% of our guests make it to the top of Africa’s highest mountain. There is a lot you can do to improve your odds of reaching the summit and to make sure you enjoy the trek. Buying good gear and doing practice hikes at home a few months before you leave is one of them. As altitude sickness is the main reason people fail to reach the top, it is important not to rush. Let your body acclimatise. Below you can see which climbing route suits you best.

Best route for climbing Kilimanjaro?

Once you have made up your mind to climb Kilimanjaro, the big question is which route to take. Seven different routes lead to the summit, and they differ in length, difficulty, scenery, traffic, accommodation and more. Based on our experience, we recommend picking one of the first 3 routes.

  • Machame route
    The variety and beauty of this route make it one of the most popular climbs. You walk from green rainforest to the lovely Shira Plateau and the lava-rock slopes. The trail curves around the mountain and offers spectacular views. A further plus is the gradual ascent, which gives people more time to acclimatise and so raises the success rate. The Machame route, also called the “Whiskey route”, has an average to high success rate, particularly on the 7-day itinerary. It costs less than most other routes, but the downside is that it can be rather busy.
  • Lemosho route
    The Lemosho route is famous for its varied and breathtaking scenery. It also sees fewer climbers than some of the longer-established routes on Kilimanjaro. Although Memosho costs more, it allows climbers ample time to acclimatise properly. A remote and beautiful route with a high success rate.
  • Marangu route
    The Maranga route is the second most popular and the cheapest way to the top of Kilimanjaro. It is the only route that ascends and descends along the same trail, and the only one with hut accommodation (camping is not permitted). Nicknamed the “Coca-Cola” route, it has a reputation as one of the easiest paths on the mountain. It is certainly the shortest Kilimanjaro route, but that leaves climbers less time to acclimatise, which explains its lower success rate. Unfortunately Marangu is also less scenic than other routes and can get crowded in peak season.
  • Northern Circuit route
    The Northern Circuit is the longest route up Kilimanjaro and also one of the most beautiful and enjoyable. It loops right around the mountain, giving superb views of Kilimanjaro from every side. Because the route is long and allows plenty of time to acclimatise, the summit success rate is very high. It is an excellent choice if you want to experience everything Kilimanjaro has to offer and have both the time and the money.
  • Shira route
    This is a very scenic route, but also an expensive and difficult one. Because it starts at a high point, the body gets less time to acclimatise, so we only recommend it to experienced climbers.
  • Rongai route
    This route is better suited to more experienced climbers. As you go up one side and come down the other, you get to see both faces of Kilimanjaro. The extra transport needed to reach the far side does make the climb more expensive, though. Rongai is among the quieter routes, but it is also drier and less scenic.
  • Umbwe route
    Umbwe is known as the steepest route on Kilimanjaro. Its rapid ascent means the success rate is far lower. We only recommend this route to experienced mountaineers who are used to high altitudes.

Best time to climb Kilimanjaro

Kilimanjaro can be climbed in any month, but the best conditions for tackling Africa’s highest peak are from June until mid-March (November excepted). February is the finest month of all, with high temperatures and little chance of rain. The rainy season runs from mid-March to June and again in November. Even so, some people deliberately climb Kilimanjaro in April or November, because the trails are quiet and far less crowded.

Information and facts

At 5,895 metres (19, 341 ft), Mount Kilimanjaro is the highest free-standing mountain in the world. Climbing it is an adventure you will never forget. As people say: “You should do that ones in your life!”

Facts about Kilimanjaro

A Kilimanjaro climb is a spectacular hike through five climate zones, running from the tropics to the Arctic. These zones are:

  • Farmland
  • Tropical rainforest (800-3000 m)
  • Grassland and savannah (3000-4200 m)
  • Alpine dessert (4200-5000 m)
  • Glacial zone, with a summit of snow and ice (5000+ m)

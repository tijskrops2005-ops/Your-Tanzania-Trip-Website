Pangani is a place most travellers never get to, which makes it all the more rewarding to discover! Reaching the quiet mainland beaches here takes some effort, but if what you want is peace and calm in a genuine tropical hideaway, away from the crowds and high prices of the islands, it pays off. This small village lies around 50 km south of Tanga and 170 km south of Mombasa, and remains an unspoilt paradise.

History of Pangani

On the cliffs to the north of Pangani, archaeologists have uncovered traces of small settlements dating from the 15th century. The town itself only gained significance in the 19th century, when, under nominal Zanzibari rule, it grew into a key stop on the caravan routes from Lake Tanganyika. Enslaved people and ivory were shipped out from here in large numbers, making it one of the biggest harbours between Bagamoyo and Mombasa. Sisal and copra were farmed on estates nearby, and a number of European missions and expeditions into the interior set out from Pangani.

Towards the close of the 19th century, attention moved to Tanga and Dar es Salaam, and Pangani slipped back into obscurity. From the 1860s onwards, extensive sugar and coconut plantations were set up here, worked by enslaved labourers, since Pangani was itself a significant hub of the slave trade.

These days the drowsy, crumbling town offers a fascinating glimpse of the past. The houses lining Pangani's main street still hint at its history, combining Arab and European styles of architecture.

Why visit Pangani?

Pangani is a peaceful spot to unwind. Sitting on the Indian Ocean, it has plenty to keep you busy during your stay: relaxing on the beach, or heading out on snorkelling or diving trips. Most lodges also arrange windsurfing, kayaking, dhow sailing and fishing excursions. Longer outings can be organised too, such as a visit to Zanzibar Island, a cruise along the Pangani River, or even a journey further south to the recently demarcated Saadani national park.

Best time to visit Pangani

You can visit Pangani all year round but it is most enjoyed from june until november in order to make the most out of its beaches. January and February are also good months to visit Pangani.

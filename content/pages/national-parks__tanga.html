Tanga, in the north of Tanzania, serves as a regional headquarter and ranks among the biggest cities in the country. Its population is similar to that of Arusha or Moshi, yet the city feels considerably calmer. Tanga lies on the Indian Ocean, close to the Kenyan border.

History of Tanga

The name Tanga is derived from the word “sail”, since the port and the area around it remain the heart of life in Tanga to this day. Sisal, coffee, tea and cotton are among the main goods exported from the port. Even the Portugese used it as a trading hub, ruling the region around Tanga from 1500 to 1700. The Sultanate of Oman then took over the settlement. Ivory and slaves were traded through the port until the European powers abolished slavery in 1873. In 1891 the Germans purchased the Tanzanian Mainland coastline from the Sultan of Zanzibar. Tanga became the first settlement of German East Africa and served as the hub of German colonial administration. Dar es Salaam was only founded later, in the early 20th century.

Being on the coast, Tanga found itself on the front line with British East Africa (Kenya) when World War I broke out. Once the war was over, Great Britain took charge of Tanganyika, and the city grew and made use of its agricultural potential. In the early years of independence from 1961, the port of Tanga remained a gateway for exporting the region’s sisal. However, with the government taking control of agricultural trade and world market prices for sisal falling. The port of Tanga started to lose revenue.

Despite its size, Tanga today is an agreeable place with faded charm, fascinating architecture, a sleepy, semi-colonial feel and wide streets busy with cyclists and motorbikes.

Advantages and disadvantages

As an off the beaten track destination, Tanga comes with pros and cons:

Advantages:

  • It is calmer and you will not find many tourists
  • it is a genuine destination where you can truly experience the daily life of local people

Disadvantages:

  • Accommodations are more basic
  • Getting there is not so easy, as it is not near other popular destinations

Excursions in Tanga

A bike tour is the ideal way to take in the city’s lovely, eclectic architecture. Hire one of the sturdy Chinese bikes! To the south of Tanga, along the tangle of sandy paths on the Ras Kazone Peninsula, there are wonderful Art Deco mansions; some are crumbling, while others are carefully looked after and bear witness to Tanga’s heyday of sisal trading. Close to the sea, ordinary houses sit next to the grand colonial residences of senior members of the British Empire. On the main road to Ras Kazone, the original Bombo Hospital rises up like a fairy-tale castle. Built by the Germans, the hospital fell into disrepair long ago and now shelters many birds. Stand among the creeping plants in its empty halls and soak up the nostalgic feel of an era long gone.

Tanga also has plenty of far older buildings in Arabic style: ornately decorated mahogany balconies, walls a metre high, coloured glass windows and heavy, dark doors, and inner courtyards where cats slip around in the deep shadows. Should you get too hot on the bike, find the shade of a mango tree: chances are someone there will sell you cups of hot, sweet espresso and slices of tasty kashata, a kind of peanut fondant.

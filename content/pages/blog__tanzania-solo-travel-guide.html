If you have landed on this page, you have probably travelled alone before, or you are preparing for one of the most thrilling adventures of your life: travelling solo in Tanzania. For anyone who has never carried a backpack through an African country alone, Tanzania is the ideal place to begin, and we will be there with you from the very first idea right up to the final goodbye.

People here are warm and welcoming, English is spoken widely in the tourist hotspots and sometimes well beyond them, and the infrastructure is reliable. On top of that there is the scenery, the wildlife, the mountains, the beaches… honestly, we could go on and on.

This guide looks at the best places and experiences for solo travellers in Tanzania, gives you tips to help you prepare, and ends with an idea that could open up a whole new range of possibilities.

Tanzania is enormously varied and each region has something worth seeing, so your route really depends on what you would like to experience.

Lake Manyara is known for its tree-climbing lions and colourful birdlife, whereas Ruaha and Nyerere offer less crowded safaris packed with the Big Five, wild dogs, crocodiles, hippos and more. Nyerere is defined by the Rufiji River, while Ruaha impresses with its vast, rugged landscapes.

Feeling adventurous? You could always set off on a trek to the top of the famous Mount Kilimanjaro.

If you are after classic safaris and plenty of wildlife with little travel time between parks, the Northern Circuit is an excellent choice, with Arusha or Tarangire National Park as the ideal starting point.

Best time to visit

The months from December to February and June to October are generally seen as the best time to visit Tanzania (the dry season), but the other months have their advantages too. In the end it comes down to what you want to see and how flexible your budget is. A full overview is available here.

Money and internet

US dollars are accepted in touristy spots, yet it is wise to keep some Tanzanian shillings on you, particularly outside the cities where ATMs can be few and far between. Most places to stay have Wi-Fi, but a local SIM card keeps you connected for the whole trip (even if your intention is to switch off). Data is fast in the cities and along the coast, and somewhat patchy in the national parks. SIM cards from Vodacom, Airtel or Tigo are sold at the airport and in town; remember to bring your passport.

Is Tanzania safe for women travelling alone?

On the whole, Tanzania is safe for solo female travellers, particularly in well-visited areas such as Arusha, Zanzibar and the northern safari parks. As anywhere, a few sensible precautions make your trip safer still. And for the record, these tips are meant for every solo traveller. 😉

  • Try to avoid exploring after dark.

  • Keep valuables in a cross-body bag and store important items in your room safe.

  • Stay alert in busy places like markets and bus stations.

  • Get a local SIM card to stay connected and navigate easily (offline maps are a lifesaver).

  • Avoid isolated areas or beaches at night, even in touristy spots.

  • Dress modestly, especially in Dar es Salaam and Stone Town.

  • Be respectful of local culture, and trust your instincts.

Stick to these simple guidelines and you can enjoy Tanzania with confidence. With so much to see and do in this East African gem, a solo trip here can be truly unforgettable.

Travelling to Zanzibar alone

Is Zanzibar just for families and honeymooners? Not at all! The island has lots to offer solo travellers. Snorkelling, water sports, lazing on the beach with your toes in the sand, a cocktail with a tiny umbrella in it… there is something here for everyone.

Things to do in Zanzibar on your own

On Zanzibar’s east coast, Jambiani and Paje are ideal for beach life in abundance. Plenty of small restaurants, bars and live music create the right mood for unwinding and perhaps making new friends (even if only for a day). You could also visit the Kua Cave, join a cooking class or head out on a bike. And of course the sea is always there, full of colourful fish waiting to be met.

Keen to discover Stone Town? Simply join a guided tour of this diverse city and hear about its history, its culture and some fun facts along the way. Pair it with a spice farm tour and you have a day to remember.

The challenges of solo travel

Like everything in life, travelling solo brings a few challenges with it. Here is a brief overview of what to bear in mind when you go it alone:

  1. Normally you arrange everything yourself, from safaris to transfers and permits – though not when you travel with Your Tanzania Trip 😉
  2. It can cost a little more than travelling in a group.
  3. At first, some areas may feel less familiar or less safe.
  4. You can drive through certain parks by yourself, but frankly it gets rather dull.

Visa Tanzania

Do I need a visa?

Once your Tanzania safari or Zanzibar holiday is booked, your next step is applying for a tourist visa. How do you apply, and where? Which documents are required? Below you’ll find answers to all of these questions. You’ll soon notice that a Tanzanian visa is fairly straightforward to obtain.

Worth knowing: for a trip to Tanzania, you can arrange your visa either online or when you arrive.

The price of a Tanzanian visa varies a little depending on your nationality. Coming from Europe? You’ll pay a fee of 50 USD. If you hold US citizenship, you need a multi-entry visa, which costs 100 USD. Both last three months, but with the multi-entry visa you can leave the country and return on the same visa within one year.

These prices apply to visas bought online. When you apply on arrival, the charge is 50 USD or 50 EUR (or 100 USD for the multi-entry visa), even though 50 EUR is clearly not the same as 50 USD. You can settle the fee in cash or by credit card – in that case you pay 50 or 100 USD plus credit card charges. It’s up to you.

The Tanzanian government would rather you applied online, and to be honest, doing so has its advantages:

  • It is a fairly simple process, particularly with the explanatory PDF you receive when you book with us.
  • When you get on your flight, your visa is already sorted and paid.
  • On arrival, there’s just one counter to queue at for your visa stamp.

Online applications are made through the Tanzanian government’s official portal. Please do NOT use any other website, as these are either pricier or fraudulent. Several travellers we know have lost money to such scams, so better safe than sorry. You’ll find the official portal here: https://visa.immigration.go.tz/.

If you book with us, a guide will take you through the process, and our travel consultants are always happy to advise. We can’t submit the visa application for you, but we can make sure everything goes smoothly. After you apply online, processing and approval can take as long as two weeks. Be sure to carry a printed or downloaded copy of your visa when you set off. With an online visa, the only queue is at the immigration counter, where your passport gets stamped. After that you pick up your luggage and you’re on your way. Simple!

Can’t or would rather not apply online? A visa on arrival is still an option. Aside from a bit of charming African bureaucracy, it is a fairly easy process:

  • At the airport, you complete an application form on arrival (have a pen handy, along with the address of Your Tanzania Trip and of your first lodge).
  • Apply at the visa counter.
  • At a second counter you pay the visa fee, either in cash or by credit card.
  • At a third counter, your passport receives its visa stamp.
  • Get your bags, and you’re good to go.

So a visa on arrival means three queues, whereas an online application means only one when you land. The traditional route may therefore take a little longer. Then again, you’re now in the land of “pole pole” – Swahili for “slowly, slowly” – so you might as well get used to it. And between ourselves: so many travellers apply online nowadays that the single queue for online visas is often much longer than the three queues for visas on arrival. You may well get through faster.

These days, most travellers choose to apply online, so they know the visa is sorted before they leave. That makes sense. If you go for the online route, come prepared:

  • Submit your application three to four weeks before you travel, in case it is rejected. That leaves you enough time to fix any errors and reapply.
  • Keep a credit card at hand for payment, or know how to make an international bank transfer.
  • Prepare a digital passport photo, a photo of the biodata page of your passport (max. 300KB) and a PDF of your flight ticket (max. 1MB).
  • Check that your passport is still valid for six months after you return from Tanzania.
  • When children travel with just one parent, you need a written letter in English in which the other parent consents to the trip, and you must upload it into the system.
  • Bring a pen when you land in Tanzania, as you are sometimes still asked to complete a form.

Feeling a little adventurous and planning to apply on arrival*? Even then, don’t get on the plane unprepared.

  • Bring the fee in cash (USD/EUR), or pay with your credit card.
  • No passport-size picture is needed.
  • Keep a pen handy for the application form, together with the addresses of Your Tanzania Trip and your first hotel.
  • When children travel with just one parent, you need a written letter in English in which the other parent consents to the trip.

*Citizens of certain countries require a so-called Referral Visa and cannot apply on arrival. Please check this before choosing how to apply. Visas on arrival may also be discontinued for everyone at some point, so do look at the immigration guidelines before departure to confirm the option still exists.

Vaccinations

Do I need vaccinations?

Besides routine vaccinations such as MMR and TDP, the CDC and WHO advise vaccinations for Tanzania including Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B and typhoid. Depending on what you plan to do, yellow fever and rabies vaccinations are recommended as well.

The yellow fever rules for Tanzania can be a little confusing at times, but we’re happy to help! Tanzania is a yellow fever-free country, so you are safe from this virus when you travel there. You might still have questions, and we’ve answered them here.

What should you pack for a Tanzania safari? Your complete safari packing list.

Whether you have been on safari tours before or this is your very first time, it matters to know exactly what you should take along. We are here to help you with that!

In the packing list for a Tanzania safari below, we go through every item you should bring and tell you why. Let’s begin.

  • Passport: It goes without saying, yet it is surprisingly easy to forget your passport as you head out of the door. Leave yourself a reminder somewhere you are sure to notice it, such as on the fridge or the bathroom mirror. Also check that your passport is valid for at least six months.

Where money is concerned, being well prepared is wise, so bringing a combination of debit or credit cards and cash is your safest option. Dollars and euros are still accepted in the most touristy places, but the Tanzanian shilling is the only official currency and is the one to use, particularly for small purchases.

Deciding what to wear on safari can be tricky, because you can never be completely sure what the Tanzania weather will bring.

Tanzania has both dry and wet seasons: the dry seasons run from January to February and from June to October, and the wet seasons from March to May and from November to early December.

On a dry season safari, rain is unlikely, but packing a couple of jackets just in case is sensible. Apart from that, light clothes are enough. In the wet seasons you’ll need warmer items, such as scarves and thicker jackets.

There are also some basic clothes you will want to pack regardless of the season you travel in, such as the following:

There may be quite a bit of walking on your safari (particularly on a walking safari), so comfortable shoes are essential.

When to visit Tanzania?

When to visit Tanzania depends on what you would like to see, where you want to travel, your budget and your interests. Every season has advantages and drawbacks. This guide will help you choose the right time for your Tanzania adventure. And before we forget… everyone is different, so there is no single right answer. One thing is certain, though: Tanzania is beautiful all year round!

Below are answers to some frequently asked questions. For more detail, read our article “When is the best time to visit Tanzania?”

June to October & December to February
These are the very best months to visit Tanzania, which is why we rate them “Excellent”. June to October is nature’s headline season: sunshine, plenty of wildlife and the epic Great Wildebeest Migration. December to February is a real safari treat, with lush green scenery and adorable young animals all around.

That said, March, April, May and November are good months too – particularly if you love vivid greenery, birdwatching and having the park almost to yourself. Some rain is possible, but not constantly, and it remains a great time to travel to Tanzania.

Good news: the impressive Great Wildebeest Migration can be seen all year round as it moves between Kenya’s Maasai Mara and Tanzania’s Serengeti. Whenever you travel, you won’t miss this remarkable spectacle. Here you’ll find an overview of when and where to see the highlights, so you can decide what matters most to you!

Tanzania weather

The weather in Tanzania differs by region, yet there is always a good time to go! The coast is the hottest and most humid, while the north and the highlands are cooler because they lie higher. Daytime temperatures usually range from 22 °C to 31 °C, falling to between 10 °C and 22 °C at night. Being close to the Equator, Zanzibar’s beaches have a clearly tropical climate.

Tanzania has two seasons a year: the wet season and the dry season. The wet (or rainy) season lasts from the end of October all the way to December, and again from the end of March to early June. These periods are known as the “short rains” and the “long rains”. The tropical showers usually begin in the late afternoon and carry on through the night until early morning. Africa’s rains are famous around the world, and watching them on a trip to Tanzania is a calming, magical experience!

Certainly. National Geographic, Discovery and other large media companies even pick the rainy season deliberately to film their wildlife documentaries in Tanzania. The weather is excellent all year, and although heavy rain falls most afternoons in the rainy season, the landscape comes alive. Nature turns colourful, the grass is lush and green, lovely flowers bloom, animals have plenty to eat, temperatures stay pleasantly cool and, best of all, Tanzania’s main highlights see far fewer tourists.

A further advantage of a rainy-season trip is that safaris and accommodation cost less. The downsides? Some accommodations close, and roads can be in bad shape. Luckily, this applies mostly to the roads in the national parks of South Tanzania.

If you travel to Tanzania in the rainy season, these national parks are open and ready to welcome you: Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater, Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Lake Natron and Arusha National Park.

Some national parks are best skipped in the wet season, including Nyerere, Ruaha, Mikumi and Udzungwa. If any of these are high on your list for Tanzania, it is better to go in the dry season.

Rain turns the scenery green, and that draws in many animals. You get lots of chances to see wildlife at its best – and at its happiest! Animals are more active in the rainy seasons, which makes for a superb safari. With food and fresh water everywhere, they roam in their thousands. The rain also shields the animals from the fierce heat of the sun.

Many of our safari guests tell us the rainy season feels more intimate. It is also an excellent time for bird lovers to visit Tanzania, as many migratory birds arrive then.

For more on Tanzania’s weather and when to travel, read our page about the best time to visit Tanzania.

Vaccinations

In addition to standard vaccinations such as MMR and TDP, the CDC and WHO recommend vaccinations for Tanzania, such as Hepatitis A, Hepatitis B, and typhoid. Yellow fever and rabies vaccinations are also recommended depending on the traveller’s activities.

The yellow fever rules for Tanzania can be a little confusing at times, but we’re happy to help! Tanzania is a yellow fever-free country, so you are safe from this virus when you travel there. You might have further questions, so let’s go through them now.

Yellow fever is a virus transmitted by mosquito bites. Symptoms usually appear after three to six days and may include fever, chills, headache and aching muscles. It is a serious disease: about 15% of those infected develop a severe or life-threatening illness.

Because Tanzania is free from yellow fever, you don’t need a yellow fever vaccination when you travel there directly from Europe or the United States.

Good news: the large majority of visitors to this beautiful country don’t need this vaccination! Under the World Health Organization’s classification, the risk of yellow fever in Tanzania is currently low. If you live in a country without yellow fever risk, the yellow fever vaccine is not one of the travel vaccinations you need.

You only need to show proof of yellow fever vaccination on arrival if you travel from a country where the virus is a risk. At present, no country in Europe or the United States is on that list. Travelling to Tanzania from somewhere else? Then look at the complete list of high-risk yellow fever countries.

Please note: if you transit through a yellow fever high-risk country for twelve hours or longer, the yellow fever vaccination is compulsory. If that applies to your journey to Tanzania, we strongly recommend visiting your doctor and confirming your Tanzania vaccination requirements with a health professional before you go.

The yellow fever vaccine is a single dose that must be given at least ten days before you enter a high-risk yellow fever country. One dose can protect you for life against the virus, so be sure to get proof of vaccination from your doctor or health professional. If you need a yellow fever vaccination before your trip, you will also have to present that proof when you arrive in Tanzania.

If your doctor advises against the vaccine because of underlying health conditions or other medical reasons, you can still travel to Tanzania with an official doctor’s letter. Present it to the immigration officer on arrival and the requirement is waived, so your trip can continue as planned.

Flying Doctors

When you plan a safari in East Africa, you may be concerned about a medical emergency far from anywhere. Fortunately, the AMREF Flying Doctors are there to help: they run an air ambulance service with doctors and pilots on standby around the clock across East Africa. For 40 USD per person, you can travel without worry to any remote area in one or more of these countries for 30 days: Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda, Burundi and Rwanda.

In a medical emergency, Flying Doctors can offer professional advice or evacuate you straight away to a hospital in Nairobi, Kenya. Say you are unlucky enough to step on a snake in Kenya’s Masai Mara: one phone call gets you professional advice that may let you carry on with your trip.

And should a freak accident leave you with a badly broken leg while boarding a boat in Uganda’s Queen Elizabeth National Park, or should an existing medical condition suddenly flare up in the middle of Tanzania’s Serengeti National Park (touch wood!), a plane with a full team of medical professionals can be ready for you at the nearest airstrip in no time. The team then flies you directly to the hospital in Nairobi, where further care is available.

Flying Doctors always judges what help is required and covers only emergency assistance and transport. You pay for the visa (if you come from outside Kenya and are flown to Nairobi) as well as the hospital and treatment costs. It is no substitute for health and/ or travel insurance. AMREF Flying Doctors can, however, help ease the visa process so that you get to the hospital in time.

Should you face a life-threatening situation on Mount Kilimanjaro, Flying Doctors can organise a helicopter evacuation to a hospital in Moshi. Bear in mind that the highest possible landing altitude depends on the weather, the terrain and approximate weight-lift limits. You can read more about this in their FAQs.

The Flying Doctors Insurance does NOT cover Mount Meru climbs in any way. Please check, then, that your travel insurance includes this kind of evacuation.

Safety

“I’d love to visit Tanzania, but is it safe?” Quite a few of our clients ask this when looking for travel advice about Tanzania. The short answer is yes! Tanzania is one of Africa’s safest destinations, welcoming more than 1 million tourists every year. When you travel with the travel experts at Your Tanzania Trip, safety always comes first. Our private safaris with knowledgeable, professional guides add to your experience in Tanzania and give you complete peace of mind. For more on travelling safely in Tanzania, read our safety tips for Tanzania.

Use reliable transportation

To begin with: using dependable transport is essential when you travel around Tanzania. It is one of the first things you can do to stay safe while exploring. Book with us and you don’t need to organise this yourself: our drivers and guides will take you wherever you need to go! And naturally, common sense and trusting your instincts help too.

Keep valuables in a safe

We suggest keeping all your valuables somewhere secure, such as a safe if there is one. Your guide is someone you can trust – he will give you the best advice on keeping your belongings safe.

Wear simple clothing and jewellery

As anywhere in the world, tourist spots are known for pickpockets. Simple, understated clothing is therefore a good choice so you don’t stand out as a target. As mentioned above, it is best to leave valuables such as jewellery in a safe while you are out and about.

Stay in tourist-friendly areas

Want to be sure the places you visit are the right (and safe!) ones? Take time to plan and research before your trip. Our travel consultants are happy to help!

Stay connected

We recommend staying connected throughout your trip. Keep your mobile phone with you at all times and let your provider know about your trip so you can use mobile data as normal… or, even simpler, get a local sim card or an e-sim.

On day trips you may be away from your accommodation for many hours, which is when a power bank is useful. If you don’t have one, or leave it in your hotel room, you can charge your phone in our safari jeeps as well.

Although Tanzania is regarded as a safe destination, there are a few areas we advise against visiting – particularly if you are travelling alone.

To start with, stay away from the far south of the country along the Mozambican border, where relatively high crime rates have been reported in recent years. Fortunately, there are no tourist attractions anywhere close to this part of Tanzania, so you should have no reason to go to the far south.

Another safety tip: stay alert when you explore the capital, Dar es Salaam. It is certainly not somewhere you need to avoid altogether, but it does have a higher crime rate than other popular parts of the country, especially for assault, vandalism and theft.

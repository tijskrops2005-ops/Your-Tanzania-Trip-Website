Tanzania sits in the southern half of the African continent and is a remarkably versatile place for a holiday. Whether you like to spend your time away being active, or you simply want a good book and a quiet spot on the sand, this beautiful country has something for you.

And since beaches came up: were you aware that some of the world’s most breathtaking stretches of white sand lie in Tanzania? Let’s explore the finest ones.

The 7 most beautiful beaches in Tanzania

Paje Beach and Jambiani Beach

Many people regard Paje Beach and Jambiani Beach as Zanzibar’s finest beaches. With white sand, crystal blue water, a range of bars and restaurants on hand and plenty of places to stay, they are hard to pass up. Yet none of that is really the main attraction.

For anyone into kite surfing, Paje Beach is a true paradise: thanks to favourable winds, shallow water and an absence of sea urchins, thousands of enthusiasts from across the globe come here every year. If kitesurfing is your thing too, the best time to plan your holiday is June to August or December to February, when the winds are at their best. And who would not want to escape the cold, dark winter that most places have at that time of year?

A tip from us: visit at high tide to get the most out of these beaches.

Ras Kutani Beach 

On the Tanzanian mainland, not far from Dar es Salaam (the country’s largest city), you will find the beautiful Ras Kutani Beach. When the buzz of the city gets too much, or when you simply want to soak up Tanzania’s beauty, this high-end lodge is an ideal spot for a quick getaway.

If you would rather keep busy on holiday, Ras Kutani Beach has a range of activities to pick from: snorkelling, which gives you the chance to explore The Chyko’s shipwreck, bodyboarding, or an outing to the tropical forest close by where you can watch various species of monkey.

Nungwi Beach 

Nungwi Beach, at the northern tip of Zanzibar, is arguably one of Tanzania’s most popular beaches, and it is easy to see why. Its white sand and crystal clear water make it perfect for anyone who likes to unwind with beautiful views all around.

The shoreline here is rockier, so at high tide only a handful of spots remain where you can still sit on the beach. The views more than make up for it, though: from Nungwi you can watch both the sunrise and the sunset over the ocean.

Because there are no coral reefs right by the beach, it suits surfers and windsurfers well. It’s also a convenient starting point for snorkelling and dolphin trips.

Add in a selection of restaurants serving traditional dishes, bars and a number of places to sleep in the surrounding area, and Nungwi Beach is certainly one to keep in mind when you are hunting for the best beaches in Tanzania.

 

Fanjove Island

Fanjove Island is the smallest of the islands in the Songo Songo Archipelago and a wonderful choice if you want a genuinely remote adventure. With only six bandas on the island, you will have (almost) nothing around you but nature!

For a long time nobody lived on Fanjove Island, apart from a few fishermen who occasionally used it as a base. That is why its plants and animals have survived intact. Visitors today can enjoy abundant wildlife: green turtles, spinner dolphins, seabirds, coconut crabs, tropical fish and humpback whales, to name only a few. Then there are the miles of untouched coral reef, teeming with striking sea creatures.

Saadani National Park 

Can’t decide whether to spend your days in Tanzania watching wildlife or lying on the beach? Then Saadani National Park could be just right for you. It is one of the few places where the bush meets the beach, so you can spend the day watching animals and plants and head straight to the sand afterwards to relax.

Several villages surround the park, and they are worth a visit if history interests you. The one that deserves a special mention is the village the park is named after: Saadani, once an important harbour town and the centre of the slave trade in East Africa.

Vumawimbi Beach

At Vumawimbi Beach on Pemba Island you can watch the daily routine of Zanzibar’s fishermen as they cycle down to their traditional boats and set off for a day of fishing. The rich, thriving coral reef just offshore is home to countless marine species, which makes it an excellent spot for anyone who loves snorkelling.

If relaxing is what you are after, you can do so on soft white sand beside beautiful crystal blue water, with palm trees swaying overhead and an atmosphere of complete peace and quiet.

Mafia Island 

Instead of singling out one beach on the island, we have included the whole island, simply because there are too many to choose between. Mafia Island may draw fewer visitors than Zanzibar, but it is a dream destination, above all for divers.

Come between October and April and you may get to swim alongside whale sharks. Sightings are never guaranteed, so you might need to head out several days running, but the reward is worth the effort. How many people can claim they have swum with whale sharks, after all? The Marine Park offers snorkelling too, with incredible reefs and sea creatures such as dolphins, turtles and octopuses.

If you prefer activities that are a little more “down-to-earth”, there are options too. You could visit nearby Chole Island, where time seems to have stood still, or the old lighthouse at the northern end of Mafia Island.

More Tanzanian beaches worth a visit

Tanzania has so many beautiful beaches that there is no way to fit them all into a single article. Below are a few more places, beyond the ones above, that deserve at least a thought the next time you are in Tanzania.

Misali Island

Just off Pemba Island lies Misali Island, a good choice if you like to stay active on holiday. The snorkelling is amazing, and there is also a wide choice of nature trails that let you discover the island’s beauty properly.

Misali Island has no fresh water and is therefore uninhabited, and camping is not permitted for tourists, so you will need to stay on Pemba Island. Even without permanent residents, the island was and still is an important fishing ground.

It has also long been a spiritual place, because of the belief that the spirits of the ancestors live in the island’s caves. To this day some people leave offerings at the cave entrances in the hope of good health and plentiful catches.

Coco Beach 

Coco Beach, also called Oyster Bay, genuinely offers the best of both worlds. You are inside Dar es Salaam, so everything you might need is close at hand, yet the beautiful surroundings make it feel as though you are on a tropical island. On top of that, Coco Beach hosts a range of outdoor activities you can join, from surfing to open-air concerts and beach parties.

This is the beach most locals head to, and that gives it a vibe of its own: food vendors, music and plenty of people out for a stroll. Want to feel the pulse of everyday city life in Tanzania? Then Coco Beach is the place!

Kunduchi Beach

Rounding off our list is Kunduchi Beach, once again not far from Dar es Salaam. It is a smart pick for families with children, because Kunduchi Wet ‘n’ Wild, East Africa’s biggest water park, is right next door.

History lovers are well served too: nearby you can see the remains of an XVI-century mosque and XVIII-century pillar tombs decorated with Chinese porcelain, and, a little further away, the Mbweni graves.

Alongside Kundichi Beach we also want to mention Mbudya Island (near Kunduchi) and Bongoyo Island (near Masaki), both blessed with wonderful stretches of sand. A local boat ferries you across. Nobody lives on either island, and residents of the city head there at the weekend. On Mbudya you may even come across giant coconut crabs.

The Bottom Line

Tanzania is a varied country, offering not just stunning scenery, beautiful beaches and impressive mountains, but also cultural experiences that stay with you. The land is steeped in history, ready for anyone willing to explore it. With a bit of luck you may also have wildlife encounters you will remember for years to come.

Even so, it is no secret that the beaches are one of the main reasons people decide to visit. The coastline of Tanzania ranks among the most beautiful in the world, and the beaches on our list show exactly why.

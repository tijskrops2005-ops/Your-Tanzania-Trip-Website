Among the islands of the Zanzibar Archipelago, Pemba Island is one of the less familiar names. Even so, it is a favourite with honeymooners. Anyone in search of rest, quiet and a romantic setting will feel right at home here! Coral reefs ring the whole island, creating a superb playground for snorkelling and diving. The Pemba channel holds a wealth of marine life and resembles an underwater paradise, where you may come across a great variety of colourful fish, sharks, dolphins and even whales. Make sure you also try the many watersports on offer: kayaking, kite surfing and wind surfing, or a romantic sunset cruise aboard a traditional sailing boat.

This is how you stay!

For a quick overview, here are our three favourite places to stay:
In the Silver category we suggest Pemba Lodge – the first genuine ecolodge on Pemba, built to benefit its guests, the natural surroundings and the islanders alike. Its five simple but attractive African-style cottages are made for comfort and a peaceful closeness to nature.
In the Gold category our warm recommendation goes to The Manta Resort. This secluded island retreat pairs genuine Swahili hospitality with modern comfort. Its underwater room is a truly extraordinary experience you should not pass up! On top of that, the resort’s community work – preserving Pemba’s African heritage, restoring marine life and encouraging unique, sustainable tourism – deserves real credit.
Finally, our Platinum category is the boutique hotel Fundu Lagoon, winner of multiple awards. This small, private luxury retreat is fringed by mangroves and can only be reached by boat. Twelve private rooms and suites on the hillside and beachfront combine comfort and seclusion with jungle-like surroundings. Honeymooners are treated to a complimentary bottle of prosecco, a massage and a private dinner as a prelude to days of pure calm, relaxation and romance! Please note: guests under 12 years of age cannot stay at Fundu Lagoon.

The Green Island

Pemba Island also goes by the name of the green island, mainly because of its rich, fertile soil, its lush green hills and its dense vegetation. This unspoilt island is covered in forests, mango trees and extensive clove plantations. Around a third of the inhabitants make a living from small-scale farming (rice, cloves, cinnamon, bananas, cassava) and fishing. And finally: Pemba’s wildlife is not limited to what lies beneath the waves. Bush babies, flying foxes and vervet monkeys also make their home on the island!

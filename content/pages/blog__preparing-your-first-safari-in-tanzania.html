1. Which safari route works best for a first trip?

For a first safari in Tanzania, the best route is the Northern Circuit, which links the country’s most iconic national parks with the highest density of wildlife. If you rush across the whole country, you end up spending more time on tarmac than watching lions.

To keep your options simple and very rewarding, our local team suggests two classic formats:

🏝️ Option 1: Balancing bush and beach (11 Days)

  • The route: 11 Days Tanzania Safari and Zanzibar
  • The fix: Ideal if you want to experience it all. In your first week you track the Big Five in Tarangire, Ngorongoro and the Serengeti, after which a short flight takes you to Zanzibar, where the white-sand beaches give your emotions time to settle.

🦁 Option 2: A pure safari experience (7 Days)

  • The Route: 7 Days Safari Tanzania
  • The fix: Intense and immersive. When time is short or wild nature is your top priority, this route brings you directly to the beating heart of Africa’s most famous plains.

2. What should you pack for a first safari in Tanzania?

When it comes to safari clothing, the golden rule is to bring versatile layers in neutral colours. There is no need for a brand-new explorer’s wardrobe to look like Indiana Jones; chances are your wardrobe already holds everything you need. On the savannah, temperatures change sharply between dawn and midday, so packing smartly is essential.

Savannah Mood Swings:

Early mornings in the bush can be surprisingly crisp. On the rim of the Ngorongoro Crater, temperatures can drop to a chilly 5 degrees Celsius. By midday, the sun takes over, reaching a warm 28 degrees Celsius.

“Onion Strategy”:

Just dress in layers. Begin with a light t-shirt, put a warm fleece over it and finish with a windbreaker. As the day heats up, you can take them off one by one.

Color Code:

Stick to lightweight clothes in beige, khaki, olive green, and soft brown. Avoid dark blue and black because they actively attract tsetse flies in certain areas, and skip bright white as the beautiful orange dust of the tracks will claim it permanently.

3. What does a normal day on safari look like?

A typical day on safari has a familiar and addictive rhythm, with game drives early in the morning and late in the afternoon and a long, relaxed break in the middle of the day. You won’t spend hours looking at empty grass; the landscape keeps changing and the day passes quickly.

4. How do safari lodges differ from tented camps?

Heading into the heart of the wild does not mean giving up comfort. The accommodation options in Tanzania run from luxurious lodges with solid structures to permanent tented camps, and both offer high-end facilities, private plumbing and complete safety.

Safari Lodges

These are elegant boutique hotels made from local stone and wood. They provide the familiar luxury of a top resort, and many have swimming pools that look out over nearby watering holes.

Tented Camps

This is luxury under canvas at its finest. Picture proper king-sized beds, fine linen, wooden floors, private flushing toilets and hot running showers. The experience is immersive: you drift off to the faint, calming sounds of nature while safely wrapped in complete luxury.

Your Safety is our priority, 24/24

The camps are fully secure, with local Maasai rangers patrolling around the clock and walking you to your tent after dark.

5. Will you have Wi-Fi and electricity on safari?

Real luxury means you don’t have to think about the practical side of modern life when you are deep in the bush.

Every custom four-by-four safari vehicle has built-in power sockets and USB ports, so you can charge your camera and phone easily while you travel.

As for staying connected, most lodges have Wi-Fi in their main lounge areas, ideal for sharing your best photos at the end of the day. The speed follows the slow, calm pace of the savannah, which makes it a perfect excuse to switch off. If you really need data on the road, you can quickly arrange a local eSIM at the airport when you arrive.

6. What does a first safari in Tanzania cost?

Budget is one of the questions we are asked most often. The truth is that a safari is by nature an exclusive experience with complex logistics.

Where does the money go?

  • Conservation first (fixed park fees): A large share of your budget goes straight to park fees set by the government. These fees pay for wildlife conservation, anti-poaching units and park upkeep, protecting these fragile ecosystems for future generations.
  • Logistics in remote areas: Operating a luxury lodge or tented camp far from modern civilisation is a remarkable achievement. Clean water, solar power, waste management and fresh gourmet food all have to be brought deep into the wilderness. What you pay for is the luxury of complete comfort in the most remote places on Earth.
  • Private infrastructure: The price covers a sturdy, specially adapted four-by-four vehicle and the full attention of an experienced, certified local guide.

Travel styles and price levels (estimated per person, per day):

  • Group trip (budget-conscious): Roughly 250 to 350 USD per day. Ideal for solo travellers or anyone on a budget. You share the four-by-four vehicle with fellow travellers and sleep at basic public campsites beneath the stars.
  • Mid-range comfort safari: Roughly 400 to 600 USD per day. This is the classic experience: a private four-by-four vehicle with your own guide, and nights in attractive, comfortable mid-range lodges or permanent tented camps with full facilities.
  • Luxury safari: Roughly 700 to 1,200 USD per day and upwards. Complete privacy, top boutique lodges in exclusive wildlife corridors, gourmet meals and first-class service.
  • Diamond luxury and beyond: From 1,500 USD per day. Picture fly-in safaris that avoid the dusty tracks, private plunge pools looking out over the Serengeti, and world-famous ultra-luxury properties.

7. What visa, health and local rules apply in Tanzania?

To enter Tanzania you need a passport that is valid for 6 months and a tourist visa, ideally arranged online before you leave so you avoid delays at the airport.

E-Visa Advantage:

Don’t spend your first hour in Africa waiting in a long payment queue at Kilimanjaro airport. Apply for your Tanzania E-Visa online a few weeks before you travel and you will glide through arrivals.

Health:

Talk to your travel clinic in good time about malaria prophylaxis and routine boosters. You are only legally required to have a Yellow Fever certificate if you arrive in Tanzania from an endemic country or have a long airport layover in one.

Local Touch:

Many people in Tanzania speak English, but a few words of Swahili will open hearts in no time. Say Jambo (Hello) and Asante sana (Thank you very much) and you will always get big smiles in return.

Art of Tipping:

Tipping your guide and the lodge staff is a local tradition people are proud of, and an essential part of safari culture. Allow around 10 to 15 USD per day for your guide. One important detail: if you take US dollars, make sure the notes are crisp, clean and printed after the year 2006, because older notes are not accepted locally.

8. How comfortable are road transfers on safari in Tanzania?

Travelling between Tanzania’s vast national parks means crossing unpaved roads, but you do so in private, sturdy four-by-four vehicles built for comfort and panoramic views.

It’s true that the tracks can be bumpy, a lively ride that locals fondly call the “African massage.” Still, you will never be squeezed in. Every vehicle has guaranteed window seats, a fridge full of cold water and a pop-up roof for views all around. The journey becomes part of the story as you pass colourful Maasai villages and see the scenery change just outside your window.

For a first safari, the dry season from June to October is seen as the ideal time.

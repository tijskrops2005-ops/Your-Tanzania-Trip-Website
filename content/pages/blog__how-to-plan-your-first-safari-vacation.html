How To Plan Your First Safari Vacation

28 September 2022

Many people dream of an African/ Tanzanian safari holiday, yet it is also the kind of holiday that takes an enormous amount of planning.

Of course, you could fly into Tanzania, hire a car and simply head off into the countryside, but that would be unwise, since most roads have no markings. You are quite simply better off with someone who knows the area.

To plan how you will spend your safari, and to travel with proper, safe transport, you need professional help.

That said, you don’t have to leave the planning of your whole holiday to professionals. It is your holiday, so plan as much of it as you like – just remember that the professionals know what is worth doing.

Picking the right time and place can decide whether a holiday succeeds or fails, and that is all the more true of an African safari. So how should you go about planning your first safari holiday?

Why Go On An African / Tanzania Safari?

A safari means huge skies, wide horizons and handing yourself over to the natural world. You get closer to wildlife than ever, meet new people and discover new things.

These are the reasons you might like to go on a Tanzania safari.

Experience African Culture

Wildlife is not the only part of a safari; getting to know new cultures plays a large role too. You can immerse yourself further in African cultures and taste distinctive local food.

Meeting a local tribe lets you experience another way of life. You come home with a fresh outlook on life and living, seeing the world and society from a different angle.

Wildlife

Most of us know wildlife from television or films, but meeting these animals face to face is a completely different matter.

A herd of elephants right in front of you is nothing like a herd in a documentary.

A pride of lions strolling past, or a hippo enjoying its bath just as you might, is far more awe-inspiring than anything you see on TV. The animals are in their natural habitat, with very little human interference.

Seeing them like this is not only more honest and real; there are also no fences and no breaks in the action.

Change Of Scenery

Every Tanzania vacation gives you a break from the stress and chaos of daily life and brings you back to nature. For a little while you enjoy new scenery, a different culture and a fresh lease of life somewhere else.

The Serengeti, Tarangire National Park, Mikumi National Park and other places will take your breath away and leave you amazed at how beautiful our world can be.

It is a moment to pause for breath and enjoy what mother nature has laid out before us.

Sense Of Adventure

Every trip away brings a feeling of adventure. Most of us look for adventure beyond our daily routine: excitement, joy, and seeing and learning new things. A safari delivers that long-awaited sense of adventure.

In the middle of the Serengeti there is little besides adventure. Yes, you will be on a guided tour with a professional guide, but it is enough to wake up the curious child in your heart.

Once In A Lifetime Experience

Few of us ever get the chance to leave home and spend more than five minutes in the wilderness. For most people, seeing lions or elephants generally means a visit to the zoo.

Seeing lions and elephants in their natural habitat, though – face to face, in their own home, with no bars and no glass – is something else. Trips like this are not something we get to repeat, so they are well worth it.

Most people have nothing more than a Starbucks and a lot of cars on their doorstep, but waking up with a safari right outside is something we should all experience at least once.

What To Consider

You can’t simply pack a bag and join just any safari. Holidays need planning, safaris even more, so what should you think about?

Review Your Budget

No holiday comes cheap, and safaris tend to sit at the more expensive end. If you want a lovely safari holiday, the best thing to do is save up before you book.

A safari holiday can cost anywhere from $200 to $1,000 per person per day.

That covers travel between camps, but not your flights, and tipping is expected. So check your budget first and make sure you can afford it, or at least start saving.

Types Of Accommodation

Think about where you would like to stay: a lodge, or maybe a tent? Almost every National park offers visitors tented camps or lodges.

Always remember to look at which kinds of accommodation are available before you book!

Every national park has its own arrangements for visitors. Sangaiwe Tented Camp in Tarangire, for instance, offers roomy thatched canvas rooms with a private bathroom, and even has a swimming pool.

In Arusha National Park, by contrast, there are lots of lodges to choose from, or, if you like the style of a traditional holiday, you could book a hotel!

For a longer holiday, consider a Northern Tanzania Safari that runs from Arusha to the Serengeti by way of Tarangire and other places. On this 8-day trip you can enjoy a wonderful Safari with comfortable accommodation.

If you want to really take in a new environment, an 8-day holiday is the ideal choice. Joining a Safari like this and sleeping in a lodge or tented camp inside a National Park is a great way to do it!

Fortunately, most parks offer either lodges or tents.

Consider A Guided Tour

If you are someone who loves heading off on your own, a guided tour may sound a little dull, yet it can turn out to be one of the best decisions you make.

Guided tours are sold as a package, and the larger your group, the lower the cost for you. You benefit from knowledge of the area and from the experience and insight of someone who spends most of their time on safari.

On top of that, the experience becomes far more immersive this way.

  • Better Visibility

Many safari companies provide a guide who drives you from one camp to the next.

A big advantage of travelling with a guide is the radio, which keeps them in touch with other guides, who let each other know where the animals are.

That gives you a better view of what the safari has to offer than just hoping a pack of hyenas or a pride of lions turns up.

Remember that you can ride with a guide in an open-air vehicle and take it all in. That beats driving your own car!

  • Better Knowledge

A tour with a guide also gives you the guide’s knowledge. Fellow passengers on the trip can add to the whole experience with what they know, and with their company.

Talking with your guide and fellow passengers teaches you more than a booklet or audiobook ever could, and you might pick up facts that no book or audio tape would offer.

  • Provides Expertise

On safari, a guide’s expertise is something to value. Guides know the area well, and because they are probably in contact with other guides too, their knowledge of it is even broader.

And should something dangerous happen, they know how to handle it, so you are not left panicking and unsure whether you are safe.

When your guide asks you to keep still, there is a reason; their instructions are there to keep you safe.

Travel without a guide and this is one of the most important things you would miss.

  • More Accessibility

Some safaris may let you go out without a guide, but in many areas the best parts are only open to you on a full safari holiday with a guide. You will see things that are not visible from an ordinary road.

Guides know the tracks, the good places, the spots where animals are most easily found, and the things that will impress you most.

Choose a guided tour and you will be able to see most of what the safari has to offer; having a professional with you has its advantages.

When To Go

Something many of us forget to think about is when to travel. With Africa lying on the equator, we may assume the weather is always lush and summery.

Compared with those of us living in climates with clear seasons, that may be the case.

Africa does have seasons, though. The whole East African region has a wet season and a dry season. What is more, the timing of your trip can also determine the price and what you get to see. Let’s look at what suits you best.

December, January, February, March

The dry season runs from June to October and from December to March, and these are probably the most popular periods for visiting Tanzania.

For something spectacular, the first two months of the year are the best time to travel to Tanzania.

In January and February you may also be lucky enough to see newborn animals, since this is when the wildebeest usually give birth to their calves.

Watch this in Ndutu, or come back later, between July and October, to the Serengeti and the Masai Mara for the crossing, and follow the journey the calves have to make.

Bear in mind that this is also high season.

April, May

April to May is the rainy season, when everything is green and prices are usually a lot lower. Because fewer people go on safari then, prices are cheaper than during the rest of the year.

You will, however, miss the calving and the crossing of the Masai Mara. Even so, the wildebeest are constantly moving, so you will still see part of the great migration.

June

June belongs to the dry season but is less popular. At this time of year the central Serengeti is an ideal place to visit.

If you want to travel in the dry season without relying on the high season, this is the month to choose.

July, August, September, October

This is the season most safari-goers choose, and there are many reasons for its popularity, starting with the wildlife being easy to find.

With pleasant temperatures, fewer biting insects and comfortable travelling conditions, there is little to complain about.

The Great Wildebeest Migration is the largest wildebeest migration in the world and counts among the Seven Natural Wonders of Africa. Every year over a million wildebeest, together with hundreds of thousands of zebras and gazelles, travel across the Serengeti plains.

Getting the timing right and witnessing it is without doubt a once-in-a-lifetime experience. The start of the year is great if seeing the calving is not your priority; otherwise, think about July to October.

During this period the herds gather near the Mara River, so this is your best chance to see the Mara River Crossing take place. The migration can be seen all year round, but the Mara River crossing is among its most popular moments.

It is not all sunshine and roses, however, and there is plenty to weigh up. This is peak safari season, and many tourists travel now because the wildlife gathers around waterholes, which makes the animals easier to spot.

On the other hand, there are fewer mosquitos, the skies are clear, mornings and evenings are cooler, and it is the best time to see the wildebeest.

If you have the chance to witness this incredible natural event, you will see the herds crossing the river, battling fast-flowing water and hungry crocodiles. It is an intense thing to watch.

Unfortunately, since these are the best months to go, prices rise, so if you choose to travel in peak season you can be sure it will cost you more.

November

November also falls in the rainy season, so there is plenty of greenery about. Just like the rainy season earlier in the year, it is a lot quieter.

Most people come in the dry seasons and to see events such as the calving and the crossing of the Masai Mara. The wet season is therefore generally quieter, which means better prices, and you will still see part of the great migration.

Where To Go

Africa is full of epic destinations, but which one suits you best?

Eastern Africa

There is plenty to see and enjoy in Eastern Africa, and every country has something special to offer.

Tanzania

In Tanzania you can witness the great migration as it moves from the Serengeti to the Masai Mara in Kenya. Get the timing right, and if the rain arrives when it should, it is a beautiful sight.

Because the timing varies, though, you can see many different stages of this event.

Early in the year you might see calves being born, and in summer the river crossings.

Cheetahs, wild dogs, lions and hyenas follow the herd, so you may well see a little of everything.

Kenya

Kenya offers the Masai Mara river crossings, where a huge herd of wildebeest crosses a river full of crocodiles, hoping to make it to the far bank.

You can also visit one of the country’s 24 national parks. Travel between September and October and you will enjoy the dry season, with cooler weather. These are, of course, also the months of the Masai Mara migration.

It is a great chance to see wildebeest, gazelle, zebras and even lions and cheetahs in their natural habitat. It can be expensive, since this is a popular event and one of the wonders of the world, but it is worth the money.

Uganda

Uganda offers mountain gorilla treks. For these you need to go to Bwindi Impenetrable National Park, a rainforest that is home to roughly half of the world’s mountain gorillas.

The summer months, June to September, are the best time to go. If you would rather not pay a fortune, however, November is an excellent time to travel and avoid the crowds.

March and April make up the low season and are also worthwhile, since you can trek to the gorillas in very small groups

Rwanda

If a Safari in Rwanda appeals to you, go during the dry season. Thanks to the sunny weather and clear skies, this is also a popular period for booking gorilla treks.

Here the gorilla treks take place in the rainforests, particularly in Volcanoes National Park. You will also spot chimpanzees and colobus monkeys living in the trees.

Being gorilla habitat, this area is naturally wet, and however hard you try you will never escape the rain here. Rest assured, though, that even after a heavy shower the skies clear to bright sunshine.

Prices are rising, so if you come across a good offer for gorilla treks in Rwanda, it is wise to take it while you can!

Southern Africa

Southern Africa, too, hosts plenty of incredible events and wonderful sights.

Keep in mind, though, that you don’t need a spectacular event as a reason to visit Africa; you can go simply to see the wildlife and enjoy the beauty of this unique and much-loved land.

Many of us instinctively think of the Northern and Eastern parts of Africa for a good holiday, yet the Southern parts are every bit as beautiful.

South Africa

South Africa is a kind of African one-stop shop for everything a good African holiday involves, and its affordability makes it even better.

Consider going in spring, around March or April, when crowds are smallest and the weather is pleasant.

These months are part of the wet season, so do expect some rain. The rains, however, open the door to some of the area’s best wildlife viewing.

This is also where you will find Kruger National Park, the area’s most famous National park.

It has the big five you are hoping to see, and it is close to Johannesburg too, so you can explore the city once you have enjoyed the wildlife.

Having said that, South Africa is worth a visit at any time. Cape Town offers food, shopping, wine and more, making it ideal for a Safari holiday followed by some time in the city.

Botswana

Botswana is full of natural beauty: besides Moremi and Chobe, it is home to the Okavango Delta. The dry months, from May until September, are the best time to visit any of these places.

These are the winter months here (though summer in the US), which means a milder climate.

Vegetation is also thinner during these months, so the animals gather in the greatest numbers around waterholes, which makes them easier to find.

Zambia

Among the best Safari options in Zambia is the Luangwa Valley, home to some of Africa’s best walking safaris.

Zambia owes much of its reputation to its almost non-commercial safari industry. It has a very large number of family-run safaris and lodges – in fact, more than anywhere else in Africa.

Over the years the safari industry here has grown, bringing more safari camps and better quality as well. Zambia is also where a new kind of safari comes from, the purest kind – the night safari.

The option of night safaris is also why Zambia has the most leopard sightings on the continent.

Zimbabwe

Zimbabwe is one more beautiful country. One of the best months to visit is May, when the wet season gives way to the dry.

The recent rain keeps the land green and lush, while the weather gradually turns dry and the nights get colder.

June brings warm days and cool nights. It is peak safari time, and the drier weather draws the animals to rivers and waterholes, so spotting them becomes much easier.

Elephants, leopards, zebras, lions, rhinos and much more live here, and the country is also home to Victoria Falls.

Namibia

Namibia, a vast desert country, offers plenty of chances to watch wildlife. You might expect it to be barren, but you would be mistaken.

Many animals here have learned to cope with the harsh climate and limited surroundings.

The Namib Desert, harsh yet stunning, is a coastal desert. Herds of elephants cross it, and you will also find various kinds of antelope, as well as lions and zebras.

Beyond the desert, March, earlier in the season, can be wet and humid, while April tends to be drier. The rains come earlier in the year, and by around April the country turns greener.

Top Tips

These tips will help you plan your first safari trip the right way!

Plan a trip of at least 8 days in Tanzania

Africa is huge, and each of its countries is just as large. A week’s holiday may sound perfect to some, but if you squeeze everything into only 7 days, flights included, you will be in too much of a hurry to enjoy it.

Our advice is to stay for 8 days, plus the days you spend flying. That way you can enjoy all the country has to offer and still have time to relax.

Take A Hot Air Balloon Safari

Regular safari trips are wonderful, but spending long enough in Tanzania opens up other possibilities too. By all means go on the standard safari and see everything up close, but then try something different.

Add a hot air balloon safari, so that you see everything from high above as well. Enjoy the landscape in all its beauty from a bird’s eye view. Manage both and you will have sights you will never forget.

Visit A Maasai Village

Make time for a visit to a Maasai village in Tanzania. The Maasai are a very colourful sight, their traditional dress standing out and their familiar spears in hand.

Traditionally nomadic herders of sheep, cattle and goats, the Maasai have had to adapt to modern life over time. Most have taken up farming, and some have even moved to the cities.

A number of Maasai villages welcome tourists, and they will entertain you with dances, including the famous udumu.

Unwind on a tropical island after your safari

When your safari trip is completely over and done with, why not take the opportunity to enjoy a different kind of nature?

Some of the most beautiful tropical beaches anywhere in the world are in Tanzania, and most of them lie on the Tanzanian Islands.

Mafia, Zanzibar and Pemba are all perfect little pieces of heaven for relaxing before or after your safari on the mainland.

On these islands you can shape your itinerary exactly as you like, including the island of your choice. Put together your own post-safari trip and relax by the sea.

Be Open Minded

Keep an open mind about safaris; every trip is different, so it is hard to predict anything. Don’t worry about what you cannot control. You might not see much, or you might see it all.

The journey may be very long, or perhaps a little too tough. With mother nature, having fixed expectations is never wise.

Be Flexible

Be flexible on safari too, and ready for warm or cold weather, scorching sun or cool winds. Be prepared for mosquitos and flies, a bumpy ride and more.

Much of the driving will be over sand and gravel, but your driver might also cross a river or a field of stones, or simply head into the bush. You never know, so be ready for whatever comes your way.

Follow Safari Rules

Always stick to the rules on safari. They exist for a reason: this is no playground, and it can be extremely dangerous.

Don’t get too close to elephants, keep quiet, keep your hands and cameras inside the vehicle near big cats, and never stand between a hippo and the water.

We all know these rules, and they exist for a reason. Make sure they are followed – they are there to keep you safe, after all.

Ask Questions During Tour

Don’t feel embarrassed about asking lots of questions on safari. The guides are very wise and well informed, and you can learn a lot about the animals and how they behave.

Draw on everything your tour guide’s brain has stored and keep asking, even if a question seems silly to you – it isn’t.

Ask To Stop During Tour

Maybe you are shy or nervous and would rather not make requests. Well, we are telling you to ask anyway. If you spot something in the distance and want a better look through your binoculars, ask to stop.

Fancy another photo of that hippo? Go ahead. You are paying for the safari, and the guides are there to give you the best experience, so if you would like to stop, just ask.

Take As Many Pictures As Possible

You might think you won’t want to keep all the photos, since after your 18th giraffe you won’t be taking as many.

Still, it is always worth taking as many photos as possible, as you never know when you might capture something you won’t want to forget!

There is no telling whether you will ever get such a good shot again, so keep shooting.

You may see a great deal on your first trip, so take as many photos as you can, as the wildlife could be harder to find next time.

Summary

Safaris rank among the best and most exciting kinds of holiday. Yes, they are also among the most expensive, but they take you to the heart of a place you will never forget and may never visit again.

These places are the heart and soul of the natural world, wonders that climate change could easily take from us, so going there and experiencing their beauty for yourself is priceless.

Seize the chance to see these wonderful exotic animals in their true, natural homes on your first African safari!

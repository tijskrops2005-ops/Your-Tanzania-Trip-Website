13 DAYS PHOTOGRAPHIC SAFARI IN TANZANIA

Knowing where to stand for the perfect shot is not always easy. Together with local guides, and in a jeep adapted for photographic safaris, you set out to record remarkable scenes of nature, culture and wildlife that will last a lifetime.

Your photographic safari opens in Tarangire National Park, the land of the elephants. On a game drive by day and another after dark, you come across animals that are active in daylight as well as those that come out at night. Once this thrilling start is behind you, a tuk-tuk takes you through Mto Wa Mbu, a green oasis, before you explore the picturesque Lake Manyara National Park. Next comes a blend of striking scenery and intriguing culture: the Hadzabe tribe and Lake Eyasi, followed by a Maasai village and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area. Stay alert and keep your camera ready, because the Big Five and a great many other African animals live on this remarkable patch of land.

From there you travel on to the (in)famous Serengeti, which you cross from the central west to the east by 4×4 and by hot air balloon: while the sun slowly comes up, you lift off, and below you the animals of the endless plains start to wake, a picture so lovely that only nature could create it. Ideal for photos you will treasure for ever.

The Great Wildebeest Migration also plays out in the Serengeti. In this spectacle, millions of zebras and wildebeest race off in search of greener grazing. Travel to Tanzania at the right moment and you can visit the Ndutu region, where the wildebeest calving season brings new life into the world.

Whether your goal is to photograph the brightest birds or the piercing stare of a lion, this photographic safari gives you the ideal backdrop.

A question we hear a lot is when you should travel to Tanzania. Honestly, no single answer fits everyone. It comes down to what you hope to see, where you would like to go, your budget and what interests you. You can read our detailed guide on the best time to visit Tanzania here.

 

    • Dry Season (June to October & December to February): These months are ideal for safaris and beach visits due to the pleasant weather and excellent wildlife viewing opportunities. However, expect higher hotel rates and more crowded parks and attractions.

 

    • Mid Season (March & November): This period offers a balance of moderate hotel rates and fewer tourists. The weather can be variable, and some attractions might have limited availability (due to rains), but you still get great wildlife sightings and a more relaxed safari experience.

 

    • Wet Season (April & May): Perfect for budget-conscious travelers, the low season features the lowest hotel rates and the least crowded parks. The heavy rains make the scenery vibrant and lush, but travel plans can be affected by the weather.

Day 2 - 3

Tarangire National Park

Platinum

Tarangire National Park is famous for its large herds of elephants and its great number of giant baobab trees. These baobabs can reach an age of several hundred years, and in the dry season elephants actually rely on them for water, using a special technique to get it out. On top of that, the Tarangire River is the only water source during the dry season, so at that time of year you can see herds of as many as 250 elephants in Tarangire, along with plenty of other animals!

Activities:

While you stay at Tarangire Safari Lodge, enjoy the excitement of a night game drive in Tarangire National Park. When dusk falls, head out into the wild and see the lively nightlife of the African savanna for yourself. Led by expert guides, you discover special nocturnal animals and the enchanting sounds of nature. This memorable outing shows you Tarangire’s varied ecosystems in a whole new light. Take the opportunity to explore Tanzania’s wonders under the stars! Reserve your night game drive now!

Day 4

Lake Manyara National Park

Platinum

Between Tarangire National Park and the Ngorongoro Crater lies Lake Manyara National Park, right beside the Great Rift Valley, which is 400 meters deep. The park has a range of landscapes that draw many different animals. You can, for instance, visit the lake, which takes up 75 percent of the park, walk through a thick rainforest full of hundreds of monkeys and birds, and watch elephants at close range in the woodlands.

Activities:

Close to Lake Manyara National Park, the village of Mto wa Mbu forms a green oasis amid dry savannah plains. Each tuktuk seats no more than 3 people, and touring by tuktuk gives you as much flexibility as possible. They fit through narrow streets and can stop wherever you want. The route takes you past the village, then into the forest and along a number of farms and plantations. Over one or two hours you call in at a brewery, a local artist and a local school.

Day 5

Visit Lake Eyasi and the Hadzabe tribe

Platinum

A day trip to Lake Eyasi and the Hadzabe tribe who live there is a fascinating experience. South of the Ngorongoro Highlands, Lake Eyasi is a shallow, seasonal salt lake and a haven for birdwatchers. Look for hippos in the shallows, then reach for your binoculars to find species such as the African spoonbill, flamingos, gray headed gulls, great white pelicans and pied avocets. Afterwards you meet the Hadzabe bushmen, who are experts at hunting with a bow.

Day 6 - 7

Ngorongoro Conservation Area (incl. Crater)

Platinum

The well-known Ngorongoro Crater counts as one of Africa's seven natural wonders. It came into being two million years ago, when an enormous volcano erupted. Its fertile lava gave rise to a unique ecosystem that now holds the highest concentration of animals anywhere in Africa! Inside the crater you are surrounded by thousands of animals, including lions, zebras, buffalos and black rhinos, and the scenery is so astonishing you won’t know which way to look!

Activities:

Dive into the culture, colours and traditions of the Maasai, travel back in time and see how people can find contentment in the simplest way of living. Discover their survival skills and what daily life is like in and around the homes of their village. It is a special experience that deserves a place in your Tanzania trip!

Day 8 - 10

Serengeti National Park (central west/east)

Platinum

Serengeti National Park is famous as the safari destination, largely thanks to the Great Migration moving through it. The huge thundering herds are a sight to behold, and so is the sheer number of predators that live here: more than four thousand lions, more than 200 cheetahs, a thousand leopards, 3500 hyenas and hundreds of wild dogs! There are of course many other lovely animals too, and your guide will be glad to look for them with you. Are you ready?

Activities:

Taking a balloon safari over the iconic Serengeti National Park turns your Tanzania safari into an experience to remember. You go up as the sun rises and drift softly above the endless Serengeti plains. The hot air balloon glides at the height of the treetops, over the baobabs and acacias, so the animals are close by. Seeing the wildlife beneath your balloon is both captivating and calm, and the panoramic views are breathtaking.

Day 11 - 12

Ndutu (only from end of December to March during the calving season of the Great Migration)

Platinum
Platinum

Between late December and March, the Ndutu region is at the centre of the Great Migration, when more than 2 million animals come here to give birth. Ndutu stretches across the southern Serengeti and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area. Its short grass, rich in nutrients, makes it a safe haven for newborn wildebeests and zebras, yet also an ideal hunting ground for predators. This seasonal gathering brings close-up sightings and nonstop activity across the plains.

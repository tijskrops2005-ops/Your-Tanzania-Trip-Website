What is the population of Tanzania?

Tanzania has a population of close to 60 million and is among the most ethnically varied nations on the African continent: some 125 distinct ethnic groups call it home, and over 100 languages are in use across the country.

Tanzanians are famous for their hospitality and are generally quick to welcome visitors who come to enjoy their beautiful nature, wildlife and culture. Do I need a visa for Tanzania?

The majority of international visitors need a visa for Tanzania. You can apply for it online via the Tanzanian government's official visa website. Bear in mind that this is the only official channel for Tanzania visa applications.

Alternatively, you can pick up a single-entry tourist or business visa when you arrive at one of Tanzania's main ports of entry. Such a visa stays valid for three months. Be prepared to show evidence of your return trip; the fee is $50 per person, or $100 per person if you hold US citizenship.

Which vaccinations do you need for Tanzania?

A yellow fever vaccination certificate is only compulsory for travellers aged one year and above who arrive from – or spend more than 12 hours in airport transit in – a country where there is a risk of yellow fever transmission.

Beyond routine vaccinations such as MMR and TDP, the CDC and WHO advise vaccinations for Tanzania including Hepatitis A, hepatitis B and typhoid. Depending on what you plan to do, yellow fever and rabies vaccinations may also be recommended.

As for any international trip, our standing advice is to see your doctor for professional health guidance before you travel to Tanzania.

What is the currency of Tanzania?

The Tanzanian shilling is the local currency, but in most tourist spots around Tanzania dollars and euros are accepted as well.

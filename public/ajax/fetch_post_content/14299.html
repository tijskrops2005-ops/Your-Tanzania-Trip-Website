Tanzania Yellow Fever Requirements

The yellow fever rules for Tanzania can look a little confusing at times, but we’re happy to help! Tanzania is a yellow fever-free country, so when it comes to avoiding this virus it is a safe place to travel. Below you’ll find answers to all your questions about the yellow fever virus and whether you need a yellow fever vaccine for Tanzania.

Is a yellow fever vaccination required for Tanzania?

Good news: if you are like most travellers to this beautiful country, this vaccine isn’t required for you! Under the World Health Organization’s classification, the risk of yellow fever in Tanzania is currently low. If you live in a country where there is no yellow fever risk, the yellow fever vaccine is not one of the travel vaccinations you need.

What is yellow fever?

Yellow fever is a virus spread by mosquito bites. It usually takes 3 to 6 days for symptoms to appear, and they can include headache, chills, fever and muscle aches. It is a serious disease: about 15% of people who catch yellow fever go on to develop a serious or life-threatening illness.

As Tanzania is safe from yellow fever, if you are directly travelling from Europe or the United States, there are no yellow fever vaccine requirements.

Tanzania yellow fever requirements

You only have to show proof of yellow fever vaccination on arrival if you are coming from a country where the virus is a risk. At present, that list does not include the United States or any European country. (See the complete list of high-risk Yellow Fever countries).

There is one important exception, though. If you transit through a yellow fever high-risk country for 12 hours or longer, you must have the yellow fever vaccination. If that applies to your trip to Tanzania, we strongly recommend that you see your doctor and confirm your Tanzania vaccination requirements with a healthcare professional before you go.

Receiving the yellow fever vaccination

You receive the yellow fever vaccination as one single dose, and it should be given no later than 10 days before you enter a high-risk yellow fever country. One dose can give you lifelong protection against the yellow fever virus, so be sure to get proof of vaccination from your doctor or health professional. If you do need the yellow fever vaccine before your trip, you will also have to show that proof when you arrive in Tanzania.

If your doctor advises against the vaccine because of underlying health conditions or other medical reasons, you can still travel to Tanzania with an official doctor’s letter. Show it to the immigration officer when you arrive and the requirement is waived, so your trip can continue as planned.

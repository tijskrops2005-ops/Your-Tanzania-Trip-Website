Tanzania weather

The weather in Tanzania varies from one region to the next – but there is always a good moment to travel somewhere in the country! Along the coast it is hottest and most humid, while the north and the highlands stay cooler thanks to their altitude. Over the year, temperatures range between 22°C and 31 °C. At night they fall to somewhere between 10°C and 22°C. Because Zanzibar lies close to the Equator, its beaches have a distinctly tropical climate.

Tanzania weather: the seasons

Each year Tanzania has two seasons: a wet one and a dry one. The wet season (or rainy season) lasts from the end of October through to December, and returns from the end of March until early June. These periods are known as the ‘short’ and the ‘long rains’. The tropical showers usually begin in the late afternoon and carry on through the night into the early morning. The rains of Africa are famous around the world, and watching them during a trip to Tanzania is a calming, enchanting experience!

Is a safari possible in the rainy season?

Were you aware that National Geographic, Discovery and other big media companies pick the rainy season to film their wildlife documentaries in Tanzania? The weather here is wonderful throughout the year, and although heavy showers fall most afternoons in the rainy season, this is when nature truly comes alive. Everything is colourful, the grass is lush and green, flowers bloom, animals have food in abundance, temperatures are pleasantly cool and, best of all, the main highlights of Tanzania see far fewer tourists.

A further advantage of visiting in the rainy season is that safaris and accommodation cost less during this low season. The drawbacks are that some accommodations close and that road conditions can (occasionally) be poor. Luckily, this mostly applies to the roads in the national parks of South Tanzania.

If you travel to Tanzania in the rainy season, these national parks remain open and ready to welcome you: Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater, Tarangire, Lake Manyara, Lake Natron and Arusha National Park. The parks that are best left for another time during the wet season are Nyerere, Ruaha, Mikumi & Udzungwa. So if one of those destinations is high on your list for Tanzania, make sure you go in the dry season.

What does the weather in Tanzania mean for the wildlife?

The rain produces a lush landscape with a high concentration of animals, which gives you plenty of chances to see wildlife at its finest – and happiest! In the rainy seasons the animals are more active, and that makes for a wonderful safari. With food and fresh water everywhere, they roam in their thousands. The rain also shields them from the fierce heat of the sun.

A lot of our safari guests tell us the rainy season gives a more intimate safari. If you love birds, it is a fantastic time of year to come to Tanzania, as large numbers of migratory birds arrive.
